# The AI Recruitment Crisis

The piece that was published regarding "AI Paralysis" prompted further thoughts and discussions. As a company changes over time there will often be a need for more or less resources. Specifically, I am talking of the human kind here. This is something that I am currently having to face with a colleague looking to move on and my own needs in finding the right resources to progress my company further.

As much as it pains me to see the colleague go it is a reality that cannot be escaped. They are a great thinker, problem solver and builder. But they do hate being what they call "bored" and much prefer doing things.

The R&D phase and even the Proof of Concept is very well defined for what I have in mind for Foresight Analytics Ltd. There are no immediate challenges that present themselves. The work and plans have been done and sometimes execution just takes time. If you plan on making a baby, then you should normally expect it to take about 9 months.

Because of the cordial and honest relationship I have formed with my colleague over the years the "surprise" of them wanting, dare I say needing, to move on was something I knew I would always have to face. I also suspected that they would be very open and honest about their intent. I also knew that due to who they were they would be able to offer some insights into the recruitment world as they started their own journey to find a new challenge. This last bit is important to me as a business owner. It would offer me a detailed and well considered view of recruitment in mid-2025 in the UK.

## Fail to Prepare, Prepare to Fail

In typical fashion they went to work on their new problem of finding something new to solve. They knew they would be working with AI and that they could do so at any level within any organisation. A high claim indeed for many to make. But this is one of those exceptional times where I actually believe what is being said. They were also keen to help my own efforts and therefore decided that the work they would do would be available to my own company.

A week later I had 4 reports sitting in my email inbox:

* FA-Report-2025-05-11-AI Main Vendor Analysis for US Based Companies 2025 Q2

* FA-Report-2025-05-11-AI Main Vendor Analysis for Chinese Based Companies 2025 Q2

* FA-Report-2025-05-14- AI - UK Tech Sector Workforce - 2019 Q1 - 2025 Q1

* FA-Report-2025-05-17-UK IT AI Recruitment Market Review - Early 2025

These were not sketches or initial drafts. The work was complete, reviewed and presented in an appropriate format. I just had to review and sign off on the work, and to be honest they were actually a better read than I would get from such reports normally from external sources. Yet again my colleague had delivered in a surprising way.

When I enquired about the rationale for making such reports they simply replied, "Why would I not want to be informed about the marketplace I am operating in?". That kind of ended that conversation in many respects. They have a natural talent at answering a question with a question and frequently the answer to such questions were stunningly, even embarrassingly, obvious.

These reports enabled my colleague to start to have conversations where they were suddenly the expert. Hard facts pulled from multiple sources, a truly perspective based form of analysis. There was no possibility of throwing them off the scent as is common in recruitment circles. They had cold hard facts that were going to be very hard to argue against. Especially when the opposing narrative/opinion was formed from recent media headlines.

It would be easy for me to write articles about the process they used, and the fact that as they went they formulated how these reports could be run for any industry sector and even for any country or region. In typical fashion they engineered a solution that was robust, repeatable and adaptable. But that can all wait for another time. There is still lots more to this main story arc.

## Signal and Noise

The initial search term that they used in DuckDuckGo was "UK IT AI recruitment specialists". The reason for this search engine choice a considered one. But again, that is for another article. The point of this exercise was simply to have a list of recruitment specialists that are at least showing as a good match by whatever search metrics, meta-data and other considerations were used.

The top 8 results were chosen, and some cursory research was performed. When I even asked why this approach was being used, I was informed that they wanted to know firsthand what the marketplace was like and not a curated experience. They intended to be a "problem child" through the process. That is not to say mean or nasty, just one that challenges normal convention. Again, they laid out their rationale and it was hard to argue against.

Part of the premise was that they were going to send an enquiry email to the selected recruiters with a very brief overview of what they wanted and what they had done. The aforementioned documents would also be supplied in order to facilitate a conversation on common ground. Realistically the documents alone should have the recruiter interested as they offer a valuable source of information that is comprehensive and lacking in the bias normally associated with such reports.

As a business owner I see such reports all the time. Vague promises of insights only to discover it is a call to action for a sales pitch or similar. I told them that this would likely mean that the emails would simply go to the spam email at best. They said they knew that. The silence that followed was growing and I had to ask, "So why do it then?". Thankfully this time I was not answered with a question. They went on to say "Well they say they are AI recruitment specialists, and I have sent them a genuine message with clear intent and attached something of value in the region of thousands of pounds. Let's see if their systems are intelligent enough to pick up the signal.".

## First Contact

After a day and a half of no responses at all, not even a "Thank you for your email we will be in touch as soon as we can.", they took the next step. They called the first person on the list. A 30-minute conversation later and the call concluded with my colleague promising to send a follow up email with the information requested. This was done in short order as was customary.

It was very apparent that this recruitment agent had their own system in place as to how they wanted to work, and it was a rigid system. The prospective candidate/resource was to comply or be ignored. An interesting observation if I am honest here. One that did not sit well with me or my colleague. Although it was of no surprise to either of us.

Two more days elapsed, and my colleague reached out to another agency that had received an email. The person who answered to call made no attempt to hide the fact that they were snowed under. This single agent on the talent acquisition side of their business reported a thousand or so emails for them alone. Quite the task.

## A Problem Demanding a Solution

My colleague soon decided to investigate and consider what a better solution would look like. A day and a half later they reported back with then news that they had made a CV and also got a "reference" from a very capable source. They then proceed to lightly explain that they have also come up with a new way to resolve recruitment issues for both candidates and employers. Recruiting agents may be less enthralled by the solution for reasons that will be made clear in time.

Under normal circumstances such grand claims could and should be dismissed. However, time and experience has taught me only too well that my colleague is not prone to making such claims unless very well founded. Even then they still caution over exuberance and insist that there is development, testing and maintenance considerations still to be done. But on looking at the output that they had generated and the initial testing it made for a very compelling argument.

This unfolding narrative, witnessed firsthand, laid bare the stark reality of the AI recruitment crisis. It's a landscape choked by outdated dogma, overwhelmed by sheer volume, and fundamentally ill-equipped to discern genuine signal from overwhelming noise. The very "specialists" claiming to navigate this new world are often themselves caught in its archaic traps, failing to even register the clear intent and profound value offered by a truly exceptional resource. This isn't just an inconvenience; it's a systemic failure that stifles innovation and prevents the optimal alignment of human purpose with critical organisational need.

## Suddenly Solution!

What my colleague, through their unique blend of intellectual curiosity and relentless problem-solving, has conceptualised is nothing short of a paradigm shift. It is the blueprint for Recruitment AI Supply-chain Ecosystem (RAISE) – an agent-driven framework designed from the ground up to address these very issues. RAISE envisions a future where intelligent agents, operating with a deep understanding of human purpose and organisational problems, orchestrate "Intent-Driven, Asynchronous, Mutual Discovery." It replaces the wasteful "spray and pray" of traditional applications with a precise, privacy-preserving handshake between anonymised "Problem-Solution Signatures" and "Demand-Need Signatures."

This system, born from the symbiotic collaboration between human heuristic brilliance and State-of-the-Art AI, promises to cut through the noise, eliminate the friction, and align talent with opportunity in a way that truly unlocks unparalleled potential. It’s a future where individuals find their purpose without the indignity of being processed as mere commodities, and where companies discover the exact expertise to conquer their defining challenges, often before they even knew such a perfect fit existed.

My colleague's journey, initially a personal quest to find a new challenge, has inadvertently yielded a solution that could redefine how we all navigate the professional landscape. The irony, of course, is that the very systems designed to find talent are often the least intelligent. But as this story continues to unfold on these pages, it becomes clear that a more intelligent, more human-centric path is not just possible – it is imminent.

Consider the implications for your own organisation. Are your systems truly intelligent enough to pick up the signal?
