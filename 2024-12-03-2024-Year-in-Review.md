# 2024 Year in Review

OK, so here we go. 2024 is almost done so having a quick review of the past year is likely needed, especially given all the developments in AI and surrounding technologies.

If I only had one word to describe the year as a whole, it would be "exhausting". If I had two words, it would be "really exhausting". As much as the word may invoke negative connotations this is not entirely true. An awful lot has happened, a surprising amount if I am totally honest about it. Initially I was tempted to review my predictions for 2024. At the time I originally wrote them I felt that one or two were quite bold, with the rest being progressively more tame. These predictions were both very right and wrong at the same time.

The predictions I made all came to bare. Not quite in the way I envisaged in all senses, as I was expecting more legal drama than has been seen. I think that this may partly be due to everyone not wanting to lock themselves out of scraping up as much of the internet as they can. Once all the major players think that this has been achieved we may see more movement to protecting various intellectual property. Although the reality is likely closer to just being the marketplace moving slowly to adapt. Uncertainty does increase caution.

Also from the legal realm, I was rather hoping to see someone use an AI LLM to generate text that was actually material pulled from someone else who had not yet published their work that was in essence copied. This would mean you could be sued for copyright infringement by someone who got your work from the output generated against an LLM prompt. They simply published the work before you did. I should note that the terms and conditions of the AI LLM provider may make this entirely possible. Add into the fact that the courts are as yet unwilling to let AI be considered an author/publisher. This could easily change and also a work can be published by a human no matter how it was generated, and it would fall under copyright protection in all the major legal systems I can immediately think of.

## I Was Wrong

So rather than look at where I was right, I am more interested in reflecting where I was wrong. The speed of progress in certain domains was very surprising. Better visual and audio were expected but nowhere near to the degree that we have seen. Does some of it all feel kind of the same? Of course it does. But that is the "AI art" you notice. The really good AI generated content will just pass as real. If we were to be totally honest about it a lot of poor AI content will still pass as real for many. I know that I am frequently fooled when in a hurry or quickly glancing at something.

Truth was something I was expecting to feature throughout the year. Especially given it being an election year for the United States. AI was going to play an important part of how people view the World. With it being so easy to create "convincing content" there were going to be plenty willing to lap up the lowest of media slop should it suit them. The bit that surprised me more however was the seeming reluctance of all forms of media in embracing AI. They could have the smartest most astute and charismatic of presenters with AI backed research and fact checking skills but instead they have... well... . Pick your poison to be quite honest as I think it is fair to say that media as a whole is revealing its true nature in its actions. It is not interested in informing viewers; it is interested in making money. A lopsided fight is uninteresting, pathetic even. But a "closely contested battle that is going right to the wire" and "just who knows what can happen next" drum being beaten at every opportunity makes far more compelling viewing. Obvious I know, but sometimes the obvious needs repeating lest we forget. This also applies to other fields of endeavour such as with academic studies. I know that there are tools being built to inspect and validate published papers, but I was expecting the impact to be greater than it has so far.

Performance improvements within LLMs and the ability to run on smaller form factors has also been impressive. Again, I expected these but not quite to the extent that we have seen. There has also been an increase in the number of frameworks and tooling in making more complex MMMs (Multi-Modal Models) with various approaches to concepts such as MoE (Mixture of Experts) and CoT (Chain of Thought). The notion of agency has also been explored to a far greater degree than I had anticipated. But when you have fine-tuned agentic systems that perform as well as World experts it gives you a certain amount of confidence.

I was expecting more from Boston Dynamics. I know that they have it. I just want to see more of it. Some of the real-World frames that exist have become very impressive. Robotic quadrupeds, drone technology and more have all come into the general consumer marketplace. I cannot wait to see how this goes forwards, despite knowing that there will be at least for a while longer military consideration.

As already mentioned, legislation is an area I was expecting more activity in. This relates more to the general idea of AI systems and just how prevalent they may be in a society. This can likely be best summarised by governments waiting for commerce to tell them what to do and how to handle AI development and use. There have been some moves but frequently little more than guidance or advisories. The tail continues to wag the dog.

## Bits I Missed Completely

I was not expecting AI to feature quite as they have in the Nobel Prizes, specifically Physics and Chemistry. Many other areas of science have also greatly benefitted from diligent use of AI in all its guises. The Math Olympiad now has AIs achieving Bronze Medal level, which is insanely good. The surprising bit was that computer science got some recognition where it is not even deemed worthy of an award category. It will likely rustle some feathers, especially for purists. This is something that I am comfortable in living with. Such an event may even get us to reconsider how accolades are structured. I know why Nobel was not keen on mathematics but to say it is not worthy of recognition feels dishonest at best. Perhaps it is a time for a rethink. Perhaps that should be applied more broadly also by how we structure and think about our sciences.

Although I have not really spoken about this before I have been waiting patiently for many LLM users to realise what LLMs are, and more importantly a common set of concerns in just how they are created. I think that both Microsoft and Meta have published papers on errors that can occur during LLM training. This is exasperated by much of the actual processing being protected information. Just how does your CPU/GPU train the models and is it done to the same rigour as the space or aeronautical industries? Are we forgetting “Heartbleed”, “Meltdown”, “Stuxnet” and many more historical incidents? Then there is the matter of the training material itself. “Garbage In, Garbage Out”, it really is that simple. My expectation was for this knowledge to become more prevalent than it seems to have been. In fact, I thought it would be a major point in the LLM AI control being fought over at this time. But perhaps all the players are guilty of the same sins.

## Bad News(?)

There have been a number of incidents throughout the year that have shown vendors to be lacking in one respect or the other. Evasive tactics and carefully worded statements were presented as required. I have no need to name any names in this as there are plenty of results to find and I am confident there will be several lists about "Top Ten IT Failures of 2024". This is notable in that many of these vendors also push AI products as revolutionising business and technology. This will likely continue to be the case until such organisations face some hard truths else they go the same way as Kodak, MySpace, Yahoo and many others. “Adapt or die”, as someone said once.

War continues to advance technology in ways that are inevitable but also undesirable. Automatons can be wielded as liberally as supply chains will allow. Improvised explosive devices were the first iteration. There have been many iterations since, and the technology only continues to improve in both offense and counter-offense. It will not be long before assassin typed drones the size of insects will be easily crafted. A sentence I never anticipated writing. The possibilities to me are simply vast and terrifying. Especially when taking into the actual harsh realities of war. Resources and production are key. A citizen requiring extensive health care and support takes a lot more resources than a dead one.

## Closing Thoughts

For me this has been an amazing year in many respects. Things are happening that I never in my wildest dreams imagined would happen in my lifetime. The potential being opened for us in the utilisation of AI in all its guises is simply immense. This will cause problems as it requires change, and some people are really not very keen on change. But I do think that the tools of the future just offer such a better life for anyone using them. Weirdly this means that we have a chance to become more human again, which I think is a good thing for us to pursue.

There is a lot more I have to say but that will be in the article looking ahead for 2025 and the yearly Foresight Analytics company update.
