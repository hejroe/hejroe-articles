# Nobody is Right

This includes me. With that out of the way it is going to be more useful to explain better what I mean here. To do this I am going to use the most successful field in human endeavour regarding the sciences, namely physics. Now I appreciate that this may be a contentious statement to some and if that is the case then I beg your indulgence for a brief while.

## Physics

In the simplest of terms physics is all about how matter and energy work within our world. Take some stuff, do a thing to it such as lift it into the air, let it go and take measurements as the state of reality changes. In doing so we could devise abstractions that allowed us to make ever increasingly accurate predictions. This then allowed for the creation of tools that could take advantage of such predictions. Water always runs downhill is a simple but very useful rule in life for instance. Further precision can be added to that statement in the term “takes the shortest path”. That information could prove useful when building a watermill, viaduct or irrigation system.

Given the obvious utility of physics it is safe to say that it is one of the oldest scientific disciplines. It likely went hand in hand with our ability to make predictions about the world with just the observations we made. Physics would be at the heart of the very first tools knowingly or not.

## Earth, Air, Water and Fire

These are considered by many to be the classical elements of the World. They are described in works of Empedocles, but they also have similarities with other cultures. There were many interpretations of how exactly the base elements collaborated with each other. But the evidence that there was some utility in describing the World using such methods is all around us. In fact, from the time Empedocles was recording their own thoughts (around 450 BC) all the way to the 18th century a similar view persisted.

Admittedly there were also actual elements in existence at the time. The likes of copper, tin, gold and others were well known. However, these typically were pulled from the earth and treated to lots of fire. So, earth and fire could be viewed as the essence of the metal being observed/extracted.

## And Just a Hint of…

The original four elements left a lot of gaps. Gaps that could not be permitted to exist. In order to fill them the likes of aether were introduced. Their magical properties were able to explain away all the difficult and inconvenient questions that only having 4 elements created. Other oddities such as Phlogiston were put forward. This gets a mention as it led to the experiments that discovered oxygen. Mercury was also considered to have special properties that set it aside from other materials.

Laziness and belief could also be said to play a part in shoring up the discipline of explaining how the World worked at a physical level.

## Periodic Table

There came a point where the old ways were no longer sufficient to describe what we were observing. The experiments that were developed had a greater level of precision and were yielding results that could not be explained so easily with existing knowledge at the time. Phosphorous was the first element discovered where we could say with some certainty who discovered it and when.

Progress in discovering more elements would continue slowly over the next 100 years where only a further three elements were discovered. In 1789 Lavoisier creates the first modern list of chemical elements which numbered 33 at the time. In 1869 Mendeleev arranged the now 64 elements known at the time into the first periodic table.

## The Atomic Age

As we ploughed forward with our collective efforts in understanding the World, we formed more complex views. Elements were made up of other things. Protons, neutrons and electrons were thrown into the mix. Now science had a tool for describing all the elements in a similar way. Each pure material was formed of varying amounts of protons, neutrons and electrons. Rules could be created to explain certain behaviours. Surely humanity had solved the matter of matter!

## The Quantum Age

With ever increasing amounts of accuracy and ingenuity in the questions asked and experiments performed, there remained questions that could not be easily answered. Things were being observed that resisted simple explanation. Although protons, neutrons and electrons were useful in some senses they did not resolve all the edge cases being seen.

At least that was the case until quarks were introduced into the picture. Quarks come in different types, but only two are of immediate concern in this story. The Up Quark and the Down Quark exist as primitives of protons and neutrons.

In other words, protons and neutrons are made of the same stuff, just different amounts of each. A proton has two Up Quarks and one Down Quark. A neutron has one Up and two Down. Now we can construct every element by using just three components. Up Quarks, Down Quarks and Electrons. This can be further complicated by the seeming fact that the Up and Down quarks are different sides of the same coin. That is, they morph from one to the other, but the ratio is always the same, there will always be a 2 to 1 between Up and Down quarks depending on whether a proton or neutron is being observed.

## Successful but not Right

At the start of this piece, I put forward Physics as having the most successful models in describing the World. This was not because I am especially fond of physics or hold it in some special place. My interest and ability in Physics is very limited. I would have to admit though that when it comes to predicting how matter moves in the Universe it would seem very accurate.

There are problems that still exist for physics. It is not a complete picture of the large and the small. Special Relativity has yet to be joined with General Relativity for instance. Additionally, we have only covered one small aspect of physics in this article, the one of tangible matter. There are other aspects of physics such as dark matter, dark energy or dark flow that need to be considered for a complete picture of everything.

Mysteries are present when it comes to the underlying fundamentals of matter. Why do protons not just fly away from each other and why do all electrons look exactly alike? Why is 137 such an important number? What is the true relationship between matter, gravity and time?

## Evolution of Understanding

Now I started off by taking the position of physics being the most successful of the sciences when it comes to models and being able to make predictions. This is a statement that I will continue to linger by, at least for now. This is not to say for an instant that any other field of scientific endeavour is somehow less than that of physics. Just that the models offered by Physics have yielded the best results.

Despite having the “best” results the story of physics is one of continued adaptation. As with a game of whack-a-mole, as soon as one question was answered several other questions would soon arise. New things were observed, and new experiments were formed to determine what exactly was happening. An incremental approach with the scientific method underpinning the process.

There have been several significant revisions to physics in the past. If a physicist from two thousand years ago was to present their idea of how the World worked in today’s physics community, they would find themselves corrected in short order.

## Hubris and Humility

Due to the age of physics, there are loads of historical characters that have appeared as either demons or angels. Some have posited that physics has answered all the important questions whereas others have continued to acknowledge its shortcomings. The reasons for such tension between opposing views is a very human trait that I will not look to explore further in this piece.

Similarly, the same could be said between different scientific disciplines. You can be as proud of chemistry as you want but you will need to accept that it is a relatively new science. You go back around 300 years and chemistry looked more like alchemy or just plain old cooking.

Part of the issues experienced by the different scientific disciplines is that human lives are quite short. Especially if you look at the “productive years”. Would a doctor who had invested 40 years of their life be willing to throw away all their previous opinions in order to accept that they were terribly wrong? Again, human nature would suggest that at least some of the time that this answer is a resounding “no”. This is not a fault of the scientific community but a very understandable and human failing.

This situation can be exasperated over larger periods of time. Resistance to new realities revealed only delays the progress we can collectively make.

## Closer to Home

There have been many signs of similar acts of delay in other disciplines. One of the most striking in my own domain is the “AI Winter”. A controversial topic for some but undeniably there have been periods where the pursuit of AI was at least hindered by conventional thought in the field of computing at the time.

The Waterfall methodology for software development is arguably another misstep that wreaked havoc for far longer than it should have. Not for it existing or being used, more that it was used for everything when it should not have been.

There are many other examples that are present in virtually any field you can think of. At some point the observations being made present us with tough questions to answer.

Despite the delays in progress, progress is still made. Even when all the professional authority in the World states that something is not possible it oddly turns out that more often than not it is possible. The main takeaway of this piece is that our journey is more about incrementally just being a bit less wrong rather than being exactly right.
