# Creative AI Critique

I love the results offered by the likes of projects as GPT-3, GPT-J and the many others. But I must confess I am also somewhat concerned by what we are seeing. Or perhaps it would be better to describe it as being concerned about something we have seen before. The first thing that we need to accept is that such language models all work on the basis of predicting the next word to appear.

The first thing that we need to accept is that such language models all work on the basis of predicting the next word to appear. This is not the same as understanding a concept. It is taking an awful lot of previous examples and using those to determine potentially good candidates. Note I said “potentially good candidates” as the results we have seen so far show only too well that errors happen frequently. 

Another issue is that humans like to see patterns, even when they are not there. This could also be phrased as, humans are prone to art. “Today the weather is very blue” for instance is an abstract concept that some people would instantly be able to relate to. Others would be inclined to just consider the weather being “blue” as being a silly notion. The thing is that technically using “blue” to describe the weather is incorrect. But from an artistic sense it is. In this we see a “nonsense” answer makes “sense”. Many other words would have also worked in place of “blue”, at least in the more creative sense. 

The evidence of this is obvious when using such systems as GPT-3 or similar. You task the API with making a prediction and get results back that are simply nonsense or varying degrees of wrong. To solve the issue you simply query the API again with the same values and you get back a different result. This process is repeated until a result is obtained that seems “correct”. 

Is what we are seeing with the language models at this time an oddity of communication itself? Much like other oddities we have seen in the past that hinted at something more fundamental, as with Zipf’s Law or the patterns of a Ulam spiral. This is not to say that there is no utility in this. More of a point of stating the obvious, a “better” solution exists. The evidence of the better solution is of course our own linguistic abilities. 

There is no doubt about the sometimes stunning results from such models. It is hard not to be impressed by the results at points. Especially when you factor in what an “average human” can do. It is with these promising and useful results that I see the issue being with. Just as with the amazing results we were so fortunate to have with silicon seemed to make us stop looking at alternatives. This is perfectly reasonable, as if you are getting better results than you have before why would you want to stop using them? 

The big players in the AI landscape have all invested heavily into such projects. These will yield useful results. But they will also have limited and narrow use where human oversight will still be required. The IBM project Watson has become such a problem child that IBM have decided to part with it. This should be a very telling sign to those in the AI field. Sometimes the hope of what you are observing is not reflected in the actual results. 

As much as these systems seem to display human like qualities it is still very much emulation. There is no fundamental understanding of the world or the tools we use to describe it. Any intelligence observed is likely the result of anthropomorphization. If you were to ask one of these systems “are you afraid to die?”, the response you get is a curated human one and not an independently formed view. But in the moment you would be forgiven for pausing for thought. 

There is no doubt that the creative wall has already been broken with AI. There are numerous examples of where AI has got art, in all its forms, right. As with other fields, AI creativity only needs to get it right 1 in 10 times. That is 1 good result out of every 10 offered by the AI. Even this is being overly harsh on the AI. I would imagine that as the various technologies improve in the realms of human endeavour, that we will see many humans use AI generated content as their own. I would not be surprised if there are already authors generating AI output which is reviewed and nominally altered by a human. 

At some point in the very near future everyone will be an artist, composer or writer. You would only need express a few words at most to create unique and original art. This would still be the case if the style of others is used or combined. At some point I wonder if those who attend art college will realise that there is a new player in town. Especially when that new player can combine the styles of two artists and generate a work every few seconds or so. 

Ultimately the creative arts are ruled by subjective opinion. You either like it or you do not. There is also a limit to how much art can be consumed in a single lifetime. But the potential AI has to increase the amount of artistic material available is immense. Just imagine your life as a musical on a daily basis. As odd as that may seem it is entirely plausible in the not-too-distant future. Is this a future we should embrace or be wary of?
