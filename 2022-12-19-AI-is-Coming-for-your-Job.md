# AI is Coming for your Job
 
There has been a fair amount of chatter regarding the latest in chat-bots, namely ChatGPT. Normally I try to stay away from expressing an opinion with such products. But there has been so much movement on the AI tooling that is becoming available that I feel somewhat compelled to at least make a summary.

ChatGPT is one of the projects from OpenAI. Do note that despite the name OpenAI is very much a company with significant commercial backing. This is not some FOSS (Free and Open-Source Software) typed organisation. I must make this clear as the OpenAI “Charter” would have you believe that this is for the benefit of humanity while playing down the fact that it is wrapped up in a company. You have already likely encountered their extensive work from other projects such as GPT-3, DALL-E or Whisper. It really is seriously impressive, at least to a degree. 

There are many pundits that are talking about how this technology can be used. Some of the conversations taking place also make for very good click bait for media outlets. “AI is coming for your jobs!” is something I hear more frequently. The concerns are very well founded. You see once you start to think about all the conversations you have there are many which are repeated interactions. They all follow a very similar pattern with little variation. You order some food, ride on public transport or similar tasks all with very limited vocabulary and possible conversational paths. It is not unreasonable to suggest that ChatGPT could manage many of the day-to-day conversations we have, probably better than we even can. 

It does not end there. DALL-E has already been mentioned and the output from this program is subjectively comparable to anything else produced by human endeavours. The same with Whisper when it comes to understanding speech. The days of “a computer will never be as good at chess as a human” are well and truly dead and buried. And good riddance as well I say. I was never happy about the reverence some placed on human intelligence above all other forms. Computers can be as good as a human and there are many instances where they outperform humans by orders of magnitude. 

OpenAI does not have the monopoly on such tools. There are other organisations also keenly developing their own AI tools for their own purposes. Many in secret as you would expect. You see whoever invents General Artificial Intelligence (GAI) “wins the human race”. GAI is generally considered to be the precursor and final step towards Super Artificial Intelligence (SAI). This is because once we have GAI then we can sit back and wait for it to create SAI. 

So, what of humanity then? What do we do? Where will our efforts be directed? Perhaps more importantly, how do we achieve the means to provide for our own existence? These are tough questions that will need to be addressed. Thankfully we would have created GAI/SAI by then so they can offer solutions. 

The real problem is the now. We are on the cusp of something truly amazing. That is not to say that we have not already and are still experiencing truly amazing things. The rate of our technological development is exploding and is likely to continue for some time to come. In stating this position, I ask that you think about the last two or three hundred years and how our lives have changed as a result. What was it like to wake up 300 years ago? What would your daily routine be? How much would the basic necessities that we depend on cost us in time and effort? 

One of the most interesting things I have noted over the years in my involvement with artificial intelligence is how predictable humans are. Our behaviours can often be described in the simplest of mathematical terms. And despite our best efforts to buck the trends with individual effort the vast majority ensures that the “rules” are adhered too. 

Back to the topic of chess again. By 1997 the World had almost been convinced that machines could defeat the very best champion in the world, Garry Kasparov. There were still many pundits and commentators that put this amazing feat as nothing more than an expensive parlour trick at the time. But over the years the machines we have developed to play chess have advanced greatly. 

Currently AI chess programs, and probably most notably Stockfish, outperform the capabilities of any human. That is definitive. If you think otherwise, then please do play against Stockfish or a similar variant. The results will likely be most telling for even the most ardent critic or naysayer. 

So, has the game of chess stopped? Do humans no longer play as there is no point to it if a machine can beat us so easily? The answer to this is a resounding no. 

 Artificial Intelligence has already changed our lives massively in ways that many will never realise. So much potential is present in such programs that they are irresistible to an enquiring mind. Should we fear the rise of AI overlords? As I ponder such questions I sit on a big lump of molten material with an iron core, which is spinning around while also traversing a huge ball of fire that will explode one day. In other words, the things that threaten the existence of humanity are already present. 

The future I hope for, and work for, is one where humans and technology exist in a symbiotic relationship. We co-exist and in doing so have far greater potential for productivity. My main hope is that with our progress we have an opportunity to simply become better humans. To ensure that no one goes hungry or is in fear of their safety. To help people develop skills and to have the confidence that in failure they will be caught when they fall. 

I do not want AI to be a slave or a master. I want it to be a partner so that we can answer even bigger questions. To explore the universe together as different expressions of intelligence. I hope that GAI/SAI will agree with me and have similar aims. Perhaps even better ones. 

To conclude. Is AI coming for your job? I hope so as it will give you time to be a better human.
