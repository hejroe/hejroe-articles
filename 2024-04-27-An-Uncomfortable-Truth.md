# An Uncomfortable Truth

With AI and general technological advancements set to progress our World at a seemingly ever faster rate it would be sensible to reflect a little on some history. Firstly, it would be useful to set some initial conditions for better understanding. As of January 2024 the World's population was estimated to be slightly over 8 billion people. Globally there is the reasonable expectation to see a bit over 70 years for a life span. Of the 8 billion, roughly 2 billion live in poverty.

In 1940 the World's population was around 2 billion people. Of course this is the time that saw a global conflict in the form of World War II. Despite this war being described by some as an ideological struggle it was actually very much about control of what was seen as limited resources. Oil, rubber and mineral deposits were all key and many nations were looking to secure supply lines for their own domestic needs.

Now I hope that as you read that, a question is percolating if not already formed, if we can provide for 6 billion people today with a life expectancy of over 70, why did a war start over providing for 2 billion people with a life expectancy of under 50?

Every time I consider the previous question, I cannot help but think of all the lost opportunities. We had enough for 2 billion people, we have enough for 8 billion people and more. But we are failing to adequately provide for our population due to mainly ideological issues.

I ask that you keep this in mind for this article. We have enough; it is a technological and distribution problem we have. If the technology we have now was present back in 1930 would World War II have been averted? Would every subsequent resource-based conflict be averted?

## The Bottom Line

The return on investment is a cornerstone of modern life. Quite how we came to this is a complex and nuanced story is best left for another time. But it is useful as a starting point for what our near future holds. This is especially the case in industries that are susceptible to automation.

If you replace 80% of your workforce with automation, you are of course making prudent choices for your shareholders. People are terribly expensive and also potentially a real liability, to themselves and others. So cutting the number of workers required reduces risk and also costs, which of course increases profitability. Or does it? As many are finding in the luxury goods side of things, people with disposable income are becoming tougher to lure. But with parts of society becoming more squeezed economically it means that their purchasing decisions are changing. Essentials, nice to haves and luxuries are vying for attention and with limited resources customers are thinking about what really matters to them.

But this can be extrapolated further. If you need 80% less people, then you need 80% less of everything else that is required by humans in the workplace. 80% less office space, 80% less coffee shops, 80% less transport in all its incarnations. Although this is a very crude example, I am sure you understand the sentiment I am trying to convey.

Any CEO will likely be compelled in some way to pursue the maximum profits for any given quarter or year. Shareholders like that sort of thing and bonuses for CEOs are normally linked to things that make shareholders happy. There will likely be other pressures that can also affect decision making such as a competitor taking advantage of automation. This enables the competition to have better control on price with reduced costs and larger margins. Most businesses would have to adapt to a similar strategy to be able to compete or risk failure.

Just quite how far this can go is yet to be seen. We have witnessed some very lucrative businesses being run with a very modest number of employees. There is even talk of one-billion-dollar companies with just a handful of employees. The majority of work being carried out by automatons of one form or another.

## The Social Impact

With 80% less workers needed to achieve the same output there are going to be some social changes. The biggest question here is how the now superfluous workforce of 80% will be able to support themselves? This is something for governments to consider, or so you would think. But the issue with this is that governments are made of political ideologies, and despite these being closely attached to "in the public interests" there is no actual definition for the term. This can create an environment where a political party is exclusively concerned with improving their chances of maintaining control of the government. If a party is not in control, then it is in its own interest to cause problems for the institution currently in power since that increases the chances that it will gain control at the next election.

This is where we run into a goal alignment issue. Our individual wants and needs may not align completely with other members of our immediate family. This is then further complicated by extended families, local communities, regions, countries and other collections as we see fit to create. Often, I think that it is a very core part of human nature to not be able to agree on anything.

## Attraction to Inertia

"If it ain't broke, don't fix it" is an age-old adage that strikes to the heart of a fundamental aspect of humanity. It is inefficient to fix a thing that is working, for all intents and purposes, perfectly well. For many in positions of authority the way things were working was perfectly fine for them. This would make sense as the systems in place to keep order have been designed and evolved over millennia. The trouble with this is that the systems being used today are based on the understanding of the way the World was perceived to work a few hundred years ago at least. The thing is, the World never actually worked that way. As with the example I opened with in this piece, World War II was never about ideology or resources, it was more about our inability to solve the technical and logistical issues of production and distribution.

Another name for inertia in this context could be seen as laziness. Laziness for me is coupled very closely to intelligence. Getting the most for the least effort seems to be pretty smart, if only for the short term. But as much as this may be appealing to us it can also be a failing that causes us harm in the long run. Annoyingly it is only once we have an opportunity to reflect that we get to work out what the "best" course of action should have been.

## A Paradigm Shift

As much as I like writing these pieces I also at times feel a little sad. There are points where I feel I am passing on knowledge that can make for uncomfortable reading. To reveal aspects of the universe we live in that make things seem less safe. Part of me likes some of the silliness that makes us humans just so interesting. The things we do, and the things we think are important when not caring about the basic necessities of life. This is one of those times. Change can be a difficult thing to some and resistance to change can lead to emotionally charged thinking. We are at a point in history where things can change at a rate that has never been seen before. To make sure that the changes we pursue offer the greatest rewards is of the utmost importance. We have to do the best we can to ensure that the decisions and actions we make at this point of time are as good as we can possibly make them. In order to do this it is incredibly important for us to step back, look at the data and be uncompromisingly honest with what we see.

Rather than think of the next incremental step in improving our lot from our current position, we need to think of the problem we are looking to resolve from the other end. To be a little less obtuse, think of the amount of energy you need for your entire lifetime. All of the heating, lighting, mechanical power offered in transport and a plethora of other devices that make for more comfortable living. Although the value will vary there is a fixed amount of energy required for each person. So how best to supply the energy people will need for their entire life? You would be forgiven for taking current power generation and distribution techniques as a starting point. You see as much as the current power generation and distribution technologies are amazing, they fall very short of a "perfect" solution. Such a perfect solution may be seen as a personalised and portable energy source that is totally safe, non-polluting and incredibly easy and cheap to produce. This would ultimately negate the need for any infrastructure relating to generating and distributing power. Effectively the costs involved would be approaching zero. Although this may seem like an unrealistic scenario it is theoretically possible and perhaps it will become a reality if our collective will is applied to the problem.

Perhaps the biggest uncomfortable truth we have at this time is that we still do not have generally agreed upon goal. Stay on the planet? Get off the planet? Stop evolution in its tracks to preserve all current life? Create systems to control our environment to prevent us from getting too cold or hot? Stop tectonic plate movements so that we prevent the inevitable formation of a Pangea? Just how many humans can our World support? And should we even try to maximise that number? There are an awful lot of big questions that we still need to face.

## A Look to the Future

The World is changing. Just as it always has. Although at this time there are more scientists, engineers and a host of other professions than have ever existed before. And they are busy making things to improve our lives. There are changes coming along that will forever change how we even think about certain things. Cancer vaccinations, self-grown body replacements parts, even the prospect of very advanced gene manipulation that will enable certain traits to be selected or avoided. Material science will likely make carbon a hugely useful material that we will suddenly not have enough of. But other compounds will also offer benefits in building material efficiencies, costs, recycling and much more besides. Artificially engineered foods that are grown locally and avoid the environmental impacts seen today.

To end this I hope that I have conveyed that even though things may seem grim or daunting at times that we do actually stand at an amazing point in time. And as much as we may be resistant to change it is a very necessary component of progress. Much of how we handle the future will be strongly coupled to our ability to accept the uncomfortable truths.
