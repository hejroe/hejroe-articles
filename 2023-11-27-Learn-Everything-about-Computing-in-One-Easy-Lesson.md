# Learn Everything about Computing in One Easy Lesson

In this piece I am going to teach you everything about computing. A bold claim you may think, and you would be right in thinking that. You see there is a bit of a catch to this. I am only going to teach you the most basic elements of computing. Even then I am going to cheat quite a bit as I will be building on the work of others.

## As Simple As A Switch

For the time being at least all of the implemented computing is based on a switch. A most simple device that is either on or off. The "on" or "off" positions can then be associated with other types of meaning.

The work of Boole related to the field of logic. In this work Boole decided upon a 1 as representing True and 0 as False. This may seem familiar already to some. Their work also included other useful logical operations such as AND or OR. Boole of course was not the first person to consider logic. There is a storied history about the science of logic that has its roots firmly in the mists of time. This fascinating story is something you may want to investigate at some point. But for now, Boole is our starting point.

Shannon took the work of Boole and applied it using electrical engineering. Soon Stibitz created the first electronic calculator (the Model K, which later became known as the "half adder"), which notably only performed binary operations. This work was then progressed further by Bell Labs that released the mighty Model 1, a computational device able to add two 8-digit numbers together in a tenth of a second. There are loads of important people not being mentioned here. To list all the names and the wonderful inventions and observations that have been made would make this essay too unwieldy.

So now we have the concept of some logic as well as the means to express that logic using a very useful thing called electricity. Note how few things actually exist at this time as components. Now I could spend the next several hundreds, if not thousands, of words describing in detail how we can get from simple on/off switches, or 1s and 0s if you prefer, or I can just link this wonderful video by Sebastian Lague. 

Being a fan of efficiency, I have naturally gone for the video option.

After watching the video, you will have gained an understanding of how the very simple concepts of AND, NOT and OR and be combined to make increasingly complex systems. The new systems created can then be used with each other to create even more complex systems. These are the building blocks of computing. They are all you really need to start your computing journey. And that concludes the lesson and you now know everything about computing. 

## Robbed!

OK, I get it. You probably feel somewhat cheated.

All of the complexity we see in computing today is a result of these most simple concepts. Every pixel activated to light up a display, every time computer memory or hard disk space is accessed, they all rely on the same principles. Every social media post, computer game, virtual or augmented reality, again all the same foundations.

Now to perhaps make a point. Consider some other very simple components. Up quarks, down quarks and electrons for instance. Three very simple things, at least conceptually for the purposes of this piece. What can you make with these? Especially if you add a few other things such as gravity and somewhere for things to happen. Maybe even throw in some more exotic particles for fun. Hopefully you have taken the hint and realised that with seemingly a couple of handfuls of "things" we are able to create the entire complexity that we observe in this fantastic place we call our Universe.

There is something poetic in all of this. Much of computing is the replication of what we see in the Universe in a computational environment. In the universe we have seen so far very few things can lead to amazing complexity as which we have with life. The same can be observed in computing.

It is astounding to me that so much diversity, complexity, ingenuity and more can all come from something so simple. Everything within the digital domain stems from the same very simple elements. Those simple switches arranged in ingenious configurations. All enabling us to model the World we observe in ways that can often be useful or entertaining to us.

I hope I have managed to get things somewhat back on track where you feel slightly less robbed. More importantly I hope to have underlined the point that amazing things can be created from the simplest of things. It is something that almost leaves me breathless when I consider it deeply. To my regret it is something that the humdrum of life normally makes me forget a lot of the time. Perhaps the real reason for this piece was just to remind myself just how incredible it all is.
