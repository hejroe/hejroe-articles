# All About AI Agents

All the big names have been on about agents, agency and agentic systems for a good few months now in a more public way. They are the next "big thing" in AI. And to be quite honest in this, they are very right for once. To navigate our way through this new field of technology it is first necessary to ensure that we set a common understanding of just what agents are in all their forms.

The idea of agents is a simple one. It is where someone, or something, acts on your instructions by your authority. The concept is a common one that is prevalent in most societies in one form or another. Estate agents, solicitors and even specific individuals if required to name but a few. We assign instructions to these entities as they are experts in a field that is frequently complex and even restricted to those able and officially accredited to perform certain functions.

This simple definition is one that I think we should always try to keep in mind. It is very easy to become distracted by complexities, processes and workflows when thinking about how agents of all types work. But at its core there is a human desire for something to happen and for the responsibility to be passed over to something else to perform that work. This is where the potential for machines to act as agents comes into play.

## You Are in Charge

If you instruct something to act as an agent on your behalf, then you are passing off the responsibility in relation to that task but not the accountability. In other words, you could not instruct a robotic entity to commit a crime for your benefit in any capacity and be absolved of any consequences.

There is then also the consideration of End User License Agreements (EULAs) and Terms of Service (ToS) and other legal documents and declarations that are present in automated systems. The carefully crafted wording over centuries has resulted in rather robust language when looking to evade end user actions. To add to that there are a lot of laws that cover vast ranges of potential harms in sometimes very simple language.

Thankfully the solution to this issue is also very simple in principle at least. You ask if there are any laws or adverse consequences that can arise from a specific instruction prior to the instruction being executed. This can even be expanded to more than a single consideration and have multiple ones as other luminaries have already pointed out. Asimov's laws of robotics and Bladerunner spring to mind as quick examples for automatons even with their respective flaws.

Being so open and upfront about the legal implications may seem surprising to some. But it is very important to stress this aspect as there will be plenty that will happily sell a solution while offering complete assurances that everything will be fine with the contractual obligations stating something else.

It would be easy to explore and then labour this point more. But that can wait as now we can focus on the more important aspect of agents and agency.

## What Can it do for Me?

The big one being pushed by larger vendors seems to focus greatly on "shopping experiences". AI service agents that can assist customers in procuring goods and services. A noble endeavour I am sure that has no hidden benefits for the suppliers of advanced AI agents. Equally I would be certain that all the results returned will be the best possible for the customer. There should be a sarcasm warning in that last sentence or two.

This is only the tip of the iceberg. Agents can do far more than that. Want a new job? Then why not have a digital twin to do all the hard work for you? You simply answer a few questions about what you can do and what you want, clone a voice, take a selfie and away your digital twin goes. The job-hunting-twin will then go about all of the recruitment sites and agencies you wish to engage with and act as a filter. Meanwhile you get to go off and do other things instead.

Altering this idea slightly opens up a whole new set of options for you. If you want a business development manager to find new potential clients than you can craft an agent that will do that for you. They will then work tirelessly in identifying potential leads and more.

Taking these concepts further should be trivial and likely more than a little appealing. You get to do more and to do it far better than a "mere human" ever could. Every enquiry with a potential customer will be handled perfectly and recorded perfectly. Sentiment analysis, economic analysis, marketplace analysis and more can all be bundled up into the process. Something that historically would have not been economically viable under normal business operations.

More imaginative minds may even be tempted to start thinking of entire teams and process workflows that can have dozens or hundreds of agents all working in coordination with each other. Whole product development teams could be crafted with oversight from a "human in the loop" or similar.

## Perfectly Perfect

In time we can also expect AI typed agents to become exceptional at a task. This can be expected to happen in very short order. There will be archetypal templates of agents that we can just select and tune as we wish. Or even better still, our agents tune themselves to the entity they are interacting with.

For instance, a reception function is likely desirable for almost all companies. A central point of contact that can handle queries and enquiries. 24/7 for every day of the year. Never sick, depending on your contingency process, tired or inconsistent. Completely saleable and for many, the most important element, incredibly low cost.

The "weak" and "narrow" AI we have at this time will outperform humans every time in certain domains. If you collect a few of these perfect systems together you can craft an agent with superhuman like skills. Will they be able to do everything? Not for now. But they do not need to do everything, they just need to do the relatively well described and constrained business function.

## Current Options

Just what is available is very dynamic currently. The mainstream AI has moved on from the surprising results of LLMs (Large Language Models) and is addressing the concerns of a "ground truth". This can involve all manner of wonderful concepts such as errr "concepts", at least according to Meta. This relates more to creating abstract models of interactions rather than just cramming more data into an LLM. Chain of Thought (CoT) is another avenue that is proving useful as is the idea of inference.

These concepts are and will continue to be packaged in a single solution in many instances. The LLM will act as the conversational glue between other systems that evaluate and process information with greater accuracy and reliability than LLMs alone. This is likely to leave users of a particular LLM with a feeling that it is more capable than just the LLM component. People are likely to forget about what is behind the interface that they deal with and focus on what can be observed.

Services and tools already exist for crafting agents together. Crew.ai, Bolt or Microsoft’s Copilot Studio Agent Builder are but a few. As already mentioned, agents are the next really big thing, and everyone wants in on the action. OpenAI is even offering PhD level agents at two thousand dollars a month (at time of writing as I expect this cost to be reduced greatly in time). The claim and the cost should raise an eyebrow or two. The evidence and capability of such models is undeniable as was appreciated by the Nobel Prize winners for 2024 in the fields of physics and chemistry.

It is even possible to host your own agentic building system as in the case of the Microsoft supported open-source project AutoGen. Again, this is just one possible option, but it does show only too well the direction and range of choices emerging even at this early stage.

But with so many changes in the AI technology space, offerings from the marketplace and an awful lot of uncertainty making the "right move" will be difficult for a while longer.

## Mentally Invest Now

Despite all the changes and uncertainty, it is going to be very useful to understand a little better what is being offered with this new set of technologies. To my mind the very best way to do this is to dip into the technology with some very simple conceptual prototypes. The good news is that for now we are not going to get involved in trying new software, services or similar. That can all wait for later and perhaps soon we will see some better tools and standards appear. The intention here is to help in developing and honing the thinking that will be very useful in developing your own agentic systems.

Email is a bane for some and a boon for others. The fact is that email is just very useful as a messaging system. Admittedly it suffers from some terribly annoying and at times dangerous content. But overall, it is a proven and resilient technology that has been in use for decades. With how common and surprisingly important it remains; it gives us a great example. It is a familiar technology that we can easily sink time into or miss opportunities. There are definitely days where we seem to handle it better than others.

It would be perfectly reasonable to question this choice as there are a host of tools to manage and automate email tasks. Microsoft's Outlook has a set of out of office and auto response options. But they all exist within the Outlook ecosystem. Would it not be nicer to have a vendor neutral alternative and perhaps more importantly more consistent, efficient and scalable solution to the email problem? How about tying all the different email accounts and providers together?

Any business owner can tell you how much trash email they are sent and much of it is of no interest at all. But due to time constraints and other limited resources much of the email problem is just ignored as being an unfortunate reality of the technology. This does not need to be the case.

A more robust and useful email solution could have many more elements. Sales emails can be batted away automatically in many respects with genuine vendors being added to a "potential" list of commercial contacts. I mean if they are fine to send email to you advising of key products and services then you can do the same right? Alternatively, you could even request that they remove you from any directories and to insist that they do not contact you again. For malicious or illegal content, you could even report it to your local authorities as this is a social responsibility. In other words, unless you are investing heavily in email assessment and action you are not being optimal in your handling of email. To add to that, if you are spending lots of resources for handling useless or deceptive emails then that is also sub-optimal.

## The Perfect Email Agent

Taking this idea further we should cover off exactly how this can work. Let us take the example of an email being sent to us. It first needs to be assessed for validity. Does it come from a valid email domain/company? Does it contain any nefarious content in the form of imagery or viral payloads such as download_more_ram.exe? Are those UTF-8 characters quite what they claim to be?

If it is genuine then we can examine the content further to work out what we want to do with the email. Just what is the sentiment? What are the required actions? Does it cover more than one issue or department? Does it match a pattern of behaviour? Can we respond to it in a personalised way depending on our historical relationship with the sender?

But should the contents be malicious or illegal then it should be reported to the appropriate authorities. This could even be extended to passing on warnings to other affiliated organisations or contacts.

In this very simple example, we have an Email Agent that is only concerned with a very narrow scope of the email problem. It is one of validation and classification. We just want to work out what emails we have a real interest in and to make sure that they get passed on to the right people. Forming a response notification is also very useful as it assures the email sender that their email has been received and is being actioned. So, perhaps this is a feature you would also like to exist for your initial email handling agent.

This also gives us the potential to consider just exactly how we want our agent to respond to emails as in "thank you for your email, it has been passed onto the {business_function_team} for action". Just how you want to develop the "personality" of your agent is up to you. You can even try to assess the sender’s intent and demographic and craft a response accordingly, although great care should be exercised for likely obvious reasons.

The concept of an AI agent persona is far from new. Phrases such as "act as a {role_name} and create a response based on the company identity..." present themselves. With this also comes some additional questions and considerations. Just what is your company identity/culture and what does this actually mean to you? Perhaps even trying to work that out is considered a major task in its own right. Well once it was, now you can simply reference a site and ask for a summary. You can even ask a Large Language Model (LLM) of your choosing to make the assessment for you. Questions and requests such as "summarise this website for me and create a persona description that fits the company identity" is now very easy to do.

As ever checking the results is still very much the domain of humans. Although we have some amazing tools that can help us in creating and doing things in the World, it does not absolve us of the responsibility/accountability of our action and inaction.

Utilising LLMs is an important point to stress. We can use them in conjunction with a set of good questions and a RAG added to the LLMs of our choosing to make life significantly easier for us. We can even ask them what the most important elements are when considering emails at every stage. The results can then be used by us to select the most appropriate and desired aspects of our solution. This can even be extended to agent design. Ask the LLMs how best to create an agent of a particular type.

## DESTROY ALL HUMANS!

Agents are potentially very powerful things. Totally focused on the task at hand and unless well directed results can be not only unwanted but quite damaging to say the least. The fastest way to convey this idea is with an old favourite of the problem of spam email. If you were to create and instruct an agent to develop a way to stop spam, then the agent may well come to the following conclusion. Spam is created for humans, so if there are no humans then there is no need for spam to exist; ergo, kill all humans to stop all spam. As far as plans go this is likely less than desirable.

Limiting the scope of action available to an agent and setting strict guidelines are sensible aspects to consider. There is no need for your plant-watering-ai-bot to have access to your bank account for instance. This is just the same as it works in the other relationships we create as humans in both our personal and professional lives. Agency and relationships should be seen in the same context. The amount of agency that an entity has on your behalf will be dependent on the relationship that you set/agree.

This all alludes to more complex systems such as sentiment analysis and evaluation systems. Checks and balances are needed to ensure that no unwanted behaviours are performed. But even with such potential for complexity we can quickly reduce the chances of error by being precise in what the agent is doing. If the agent is to just to advise on a healthy diet, then you create evaluation systems to check that any information or action taken at least relates to a healthy diet in a direct way. If not, then some counter can be set. Should the counter limit be exceeded then you can reset the conversation and prevent an attack on the agent.

Historically the costs associated with having so many checks in place would be prohibitively expensive. However, the costs of automation have been reduced greatly as technology has advanced. Performing real time audits to prevent fraud or failures in quality are now trivial. Each of the components can be seen to be an agent in their own right. They move from process to process just checking to make sure that a very specific quality/quantity is present. If not, then they can inform one or more agent-task-management processes for intervention or correction.

## You Are Not Alone

One of the wonderful things about humanity is that you are not alone. Others also have similar tasks, problems and opportunities as you do. This to my mind is a key feature of humanity, especially in matters of knowledge. We can share ideas and concepts freely and often people do. Frequently this can result in a solution or in this case an agent description already existing to some degree. Will it be perfect for your specific business needs? Probably not. But with a bit of tweaking a more appropriate solution can be created.

The very best bit to this is that LLMs are an amazing tool that can supplement the collective human knowledge and summarise in ways that would have taken days/weeks/months/years of work in the past into mere minutes in many cases. AI Agentic templates will and do exist to cater for common roles within organisations.

These templates can also take far more complex forms. Entire business processes for support, marketing or analytical pipelines will be freely available. If you want an agent that is capable of assessing ISO compliance, then is likely to exist in the not-too-distant future (if it does not already exist). There will even be agents that will be able to bring about organisational change in order to become compliant. As we have already seen in the fields of physics, chemistry, biology, chess and far more, AI is incredibly good, superhuman even.

## So Where Is It?

As yet we have not yet downloaded any software, subscribed to any service or done anything other than describe, very poorly and briefly, our AI email agent and some potential options. There has been no talk of preparing training data from your own email store to train and fine tune your own AI solution. In fact, it would not be unreasonable for you to think that this whole exercise has just been a ruse.

If I was to suggest a specific solution at this time I would be doing you a disservice. The point of this exercise is more to show how AI agents can be present. That they are likely best tasked with specific roles and responsibilities in mind. Anything outside of that should be reviewed by more flexible systems such as humans, at least for now.

But no matter the solution that you settle upon, the actual description of what is intended is the important bit. It is also very transportable from system to system. In other words, the perfectly described AI email agent can be deployed on any AI agentic platform with likely minor modifications. Even then it could be as simple as offering an instruction of "convert this AI agent model from agentic system A to agentic system B".

Because of this it means that the AI agent playing field is very level at this time. Any company or even person could stumble upon a key special recipe for agency that performs amazingly well. Already we are seeing the signs of such things. Often the frontier of AI is described as being a jagged edge. The knowledge across all domains is not smooth and even. Some fields are more developed or have better primitives that enable better performance in specific domains. Chess, Go, Physics, Chemistry, Art and more are all seeing AI systems outperform human abilities in the dimensions being measured. This trend will continue, and it adds considerable credence to the notion of Artificial General Intelligence (AGI).

## Agents As Physical Forms

Robots. "True agency" for many is being able to move things in the World. Agents with physical robotic forms would meet that definition. We could even see the prospect of having to consider our AI driven agentic robotic systems as a form of life in their own right. I mean if you look at the requirements as defined in biology for life, then if they are all met the bar has been passed. It could even be argued that a new form of reproduction is better recognised, namely "assembly". In saying this I am mindful of the work of Michael Levin with their Xenobots.

Already there are many examples of robotic systems that have an element of agency appearing in the World, and in significant numbers. This is especially the case in zones of conflict. But there are also many other real-world applications that will see robotic systems coming into play. Healthcare is likely one, especially in the provision of care for the elderly. Not only will robots increase the potential for level of care, but they can also reduce risks in other areas, such as lifting/moving patients.

It could also be said that advanced driving systems such as with Weymo and Mercedes are also robots. Just with several constraints built in. This makes the acquisition of Boston Dynamics by Hyundai Motor Group make a little more sense also perhaps. Unitree also have some very impressive footage of robotic platforms. Ones that make me wonder if we will end up on wheeled horse-like vehicles able to off road over the most demanding of terrains.

## Early Adopter Advantage

It is very likely that those using agents for certain tasks will gain a competitive advantage. Although it is very likely such an advantage will be short lived. The Email agent we have used as an example thus far is just one type of agent. A common agent that is in demand is a social media agent. As the name implies this agent takes care of a company’s social media tasks and even the entire strategy. This could encompass the whole process. Marketing material developed directly from engineering specifications and competitor/market analysis.

This can then be directed to existing and prospective customers with incredibly low costs and effort when compared to previous more labour-intensive methods. With this comes a set of risks. With it being so cheap to create whole new marketing campaigns tailored to every market segment and demographic combination you can think of, in conjunction with ability to send personalised messages over multiple direct and indirect messaging platforms, a decision has to be made with how often you try to contact the existing or prospective customer.

Regardless, there is still an initial window of opportunity that can greatly impact a company’s growth. This opportunity will be irresistible to some, so I fully expect email inboxes and social media to be overflowing with personalised advertising to a degree never seen before. It could even be argued that not to pursue such endeavours will be the death knell of a company.

## Enough is Enough

I have never really understood the need for some companies to email me every single day about their products/services. If I was to buy a guitar online, I can end up with daily emails advising me of the latest guitar deal. But am I likely to want to buy a new guitar at such a rate? Does such frequent contact make me more or less inclined to want to hear from an organisation?

The same applies for social media platforms. Companies that “bang the drum” a little too much are likely to end up with a less than favourable perception. In other words, if you constantly hear “buy my things, buy my things!”, you will probably tire of it and even look to prevent any further contact in the future.

Already there are agents that filter information for you and remove adverts. The arms race for your attention continues unabated and quite what the future of advertising will look like seems uncertain to me. An AI agent acting as a filter for adverts is something I have written about in the past.

Solving this problem for purveyors of goods and services is going to be a tricky one. As mentioned, there is an advantage to being an early adopter and applying existing techniques and practices. But this will be short lived as it will be something that everyone can do. Soon you can end up with social media platforms being solely populated by AI agents. Some social media platforms such as Meta are even deploying their own AI agents as personas in their own right.

Just quite where we are headed in this regard is very much undecided. We could even see a return to more localised production and distribution as technology advances. There is a lot more I can say on this, but it will have to wait for another time.

## Wrapping Up

This really has been the briefest of whirlwind tours on the subject of agents. Just quite what agents will exist in the future is very much down to the ingenuity of humanity, of which there is loads. This is then amplified by humans using AI systems. A very positive feedback loop that will greatly accelerate our advancement in almost every field of science.

The main point of this piece is that agents are going to be front and centre for a lot of the upcoming AI technology vendors. They will all want you to subscribe to their own services and to rely solely on them for much of the work/effort required to run and grow your business. There will likely be a rush of conventional forms of business that will quickly lead to the “en-shit-ifcation” of certain parts of business. Social media and news spaces are the areas already seeing vast amounts of AI generated content. Your use of agents has to be intelligent and right for you, and your business.

Whether or not this all ends up being a good idea or not is going to be a very hot topic for debate. But it is a certainty that this process cannot easily be stopped. The potential benefits are simply too large for many to be able to resist. Resistance is futile.
