## Facts and Feelings

A frequent cry is one of "Facts don't care about feelings". I am not especially sure on this, but I am more certain that fact checking takes time. The more facts, the more time. This is only one dimension to this problem. Facts are very diverse and mutable things, and so are feelings. This already gives quite the dynamic between the pair. But then you find that facts and feelings are composites, containers for more fine-grained things such as happiness or sadness.

This is where we crash into the very nature of our humanity. When we are born, we are not a "finished product". Human development is unusual in the respect that a lot of an individual’s growth occurs once born. Years of further physical development in conjunction with learning and practice eventually give us an instance of a human. Each one is unique and even though there are frequently common characteristics and behaviours, there are also some very unusual combinations that can result in some exceptional performances and outliers.

As we grow and develop, we collect facts to use in the World we live in. The facts we collect and decide to keep are important to us. Important for their utility and the effort it has taken to acquire them. The obvious benefits meant that preserving that information was important. Facts were kept in song, dance, and stories. The facts we gathered were not immutable. They would morph over time as new observations were made and new proofs were required.

Our collective intelligence as a species has given us tools over the ages to help better establish facts. Methods by which we can inspect, observe and query data in order to have a better model of the World we operate in. My own desire for efficiency has meant I tended to be attracted to the data analytics element of computer science quite naturally. This all started with overclocking CPUs and as anyone who has done such things can tell you, being accurate on facts of temperature is quite important to get right. Costly to get wrong.

In other words, I think I am just trying to say that when we take precise measurements that are repeatable, we are doing science. They establish facts that anyone can repeat. Science is nothing to do with being right or wrong in the sense that many people can see the World. It is about being bold enough to present evidence in order to be shown how wrong you are, and being happy with that since science as a whole is still progressing. One of the annoying parts for me in academia is the amount of work that is unreproducible due to lack of description or useful data/sampling. But to keep things on track, data, science, facts. These are all good things by which we can better predict and utilise the World we live in.

## The Fact of Simple Appeal

In our development much of how we deal with the World is handled by categorisation. Things either are or they are not. Black and white, true and false, on or off, up or down. There is immediate and compelling appeal in such things. This is a foundational component to a human. The very act of categorisation requires computational effort. This leads us on nicely to the idea that saving energy is also a very human thing to do, as it is for much of other life. Not that any of this is an especially conscious effort. It is a very innate element of life itself. Being able to maximise a reward while minimising effort is an incredibly useful talent to have. In saying that, I feel I should also add that being able to minimise disappointment or regret is also very handy.

There are many other mechanisms that are also hinted at. Our ability in being able to detect surprise, patterns, faces and many more all add to a collective toolbox that can be used to solve future state inference problems. In other words, we think about how we want the future all the time. Much of our "intelligence" seems to be concerned with establishing a current state of the World and then constructing a desired future state. Planning and action are then used to move the current ice-cream-less state to the much preferable ice-cream state.

It is very important to remain aware of the fact that our biological systems and development have been honed generation after generation in an environment that is nothing like that which we experience in the current modern World. Every time I have this thought I also find myself remembering the alpha male fallacy that has somehow gained appeal. The intention here is to not just dash off on some generalised pipedream that is able to describe all life and intelligence, as that is unrealistic beyond imagination. It is to do the hard work to prove as best we can as much of a truth about a thing as we can.

## What Is A Human Built Of?

There are many qualities that people can possess. Psychology has seemingly struggled with this topic greatly. Psychologist have given us the idea of "OCEAN" (Openness, Conscientiousness, Extroversion, Agreeableness, Narcissism) but it is something that only has general agreement upon. Looking at the physical characteristics is equally complicated. Just what is a human made of? Over three thousand different types of brain cell alone. Reams of genetic material containing information at the microstate. There are even terms such as “Dark DNA” that have entered more recent biological conversations. The physics of a single cell alone are incredibly complex. Computation at even an atomic level is far from what we are currently able to model.

Have I mentioned the part about bacteria, viruses and other oddities that also form part of a human? That yeast infection is actually a new expression in modern dance waiting to come into reality. This may not be strictly true. But just quite where humans start, and end, is one of those questions that still defies a complete description.

## The Answer to The Problem Is...

In sticking to the goal alignment issue for humans, we can hope to use all the vast history of associated sciences to help the meagre data scientist, right? Thousands of years of human effort has been spent on such matters. Unfortunately, or fortunately, these are the really tricky problems. They are where boundaries collide, and they do so in such a way that defies Boolean models of description. Trying to answer the human goal alignment issue is one that seems far from resolved. I would even go so far as to say that for some the question almost seems to be a pursuit of self-gratification. To go further still, some even seem to revel in the chaos of people being unaligned.

It is important to note that in the human alignment problem we do not just have humans. The constructs of governance and commerce over millennia have been incredibly useful. But it would also be fair to say that aspects of both do have a number of tendencies. These are perfectly normal and natural tendencies, and they are supposed to happen, or so it would seem given the evidence around us. In fact, once you put a few components together you can see only too well how complicated things can become.

A practical demonstration will be useful here. So here is a completely fictional list of actors with a sparse set of “goals” and “un-goals”:

* Me - GOALS: Everyone happy and safe, intelligence work, running .  UN-GOALS: Everyone unhappy.
* My Dog - GOALS: Treats, belly rubs, walks/running.    UN-GOALS: Bath time, car journeys, "bad dogs" (whatever that may actually mean.)
* Child 1 - GOALS: Riding bike, video games, doing everything all the time.    UN-GOALS: Bath time, bedtime, any form of order/structure.
* Child 2 - GOALS: Sweets, not moving.    UN-GOALS: Bath time, moving.
* Partner - GOALS: World Domination.    UN-GOALS: Failure is not an option.
* Side Hustle - GOALS: To find out if Partner actually reads any of these articles.    UN-GOALS: To have ever doubted that they did not.
* Street - GOALS: No crime, parking sufficient and clean.    UN-GOALS: Road works, gas leaks.
* Local Community - GOALS: It's complicated.    UN-GOALS: It's complicated.
* Local Government - GOALS: It's complicated.    UN-GOALS: It's complicated.
* Local Commerce - GOALS: It's complicated.    UN-GOALS: It's complicated.

I am confident it is evident that the chances of every actor in that fictional list being happy/satisfied/content is remote to say the least. Even if some equitable balance is found it is likely temporary. Our wants and needs continually shift to account for our perception of the World.

In other words, when it comes to humanity there seems to be nothing that we can all agree on. Always there will be an exception. It is one of the very things that seems to make us human. I am not even convinced of the wisdom of trying to solve this problem. Would it push us to becoming all the same? To make us more machine like?

This is not a form of excuse, or any other contrived mechanism designed to reduce a complex and nuanced subject into a binary description. This is about recognising that the alignment problem is not exclusive to AI alone and that a solution could well already exist in another domain. Getting the right solution is very important. Having more than one solution to check the validity of something is even more important still. When we find things in common between domains it starts to hint at patterns. Believe it or not, patterns are important.

## Equivalence

As is normal, us humans are putting a greater burden on the machines we build than we do on any other aspects of ourselves. Do we know how humans come to make the decisions that they do? Do they even make consistent decisions given the same set of circumstances? Or what about a different device. One that is more inanimate. Do we not know the time of an analogue clock since we do not know exactly what time it is? Back to cars again, what about cars? Anyone who has tried to uncover the mysteries of an Engine Management System can attest to a strong desire by manufacturers to keep things “black box”.

There seems to be a movement that is determined to ensure that every single "neuron activation" within an AI network must be fully understood in completeness, otherwise it is not safe. This is a preposterous position to take if looking to do useful science. We do not know an awful lot of stuff and yet nothing fails in quite the catastrophic way that some would suggest. Do we need to understand very part of a bridge or tunnel before using it? Exactly!

Hallucinations and "getting it wrong" are quickly displayed to show how bad the AI systems are. A human, it would seem to some, has never hallucinated, had a seizure, auditory or visual illusion. No human has ever taken a stimulant and produced great works of art and literature. Again, these are ridiculous notions. In fact, I could easily argue that humans getting things wrong is one of the things that has allowed us to advance as far as we have. We seem to learn through mistakes more often than successes.

The fragility of the LLM models have come under greater scrutiny as their use has been maintained. Just as with the Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) imagery solutions the models for LLMs can be convinced to "see" things that are not there. One of my favourite examples of this is the polar bear image that was altered at a pixel level to give the output of a fridge when the picture of a polar bear was run through a recognition program. Humans see the polar bear and the machine saw the fridge. Fascinating to be quite honest.

## Unsolvable by Humans

The amount of evidence has only ever increased as we have improved our ability to take measurements from different perspectives. Yet somehow flat earth is a position that is put forward as being an absolute truth by some. No supporting evidence or experimentation in this. No expanding body of knowledge about the possibility of a flat earth as new discoveries are made and applied to the way we make observations. When the matter is discussed, it is between two people, one for and one against a round Earth. The round-Earther is lacklustre and awkward, the flat-Earther is articulate and relatable. The flat-earth movement gains ground.

## An Uncomfortable Truth

Some people tend to talk a lot. They say a lot about the things that they are going to do to make sure that "the people" are well represented in their own governance. In typical human nature some of us have been able to identify a certain efficiency in just saying that you will do a thing and then never doing the thing. If you play your cards right, you can even get the reward by just saying the right thing. Even better is that even if you say the right thing to get something and then do not do it, you can still be forgiven and get other things later on. This works even better if you are of preferable characteristics and likely symmetry.

This type of strategy has been very successful over the years. A competent orator is easily able to capture the moment and mood of a crowd and to develop that to their own means. Just get them all cheering and make sure that there are photos of you surrounded by cheering people. A picture paints a thousand words, right? Well they must be doing something right, right? These lessons are very well understood in history. Bread and circuses. It works.

The very fact that it works is the thing that we should now be quite worried about. Over the last couple of hundred years or so especially we have created a system that is not just about people. It is about governments, commercial pressures, and continued growth to name but a few. Many of the systems we have created have almost taken on a life of their own. Huge parts of them exist in some abstract middle ground of bureaucracy that is focused on "serving the process of the decades before....".

A prime example of this is the census system. As a data scientist I love the census. The bit I do not love about it is that when it was started over a hundred years ago once every 10 years seems reasonable given the collection techniques and less rapid changes. But today? In the 21st Century? Major IT suppliers can likely tell you the estimated current population of anywhere in the UK at any point of the time and day. If you want proof of this then just look at any modern navigation system such as Google Maps. Why does the UK census take so long?

These systemic pressures have accumulated in the various departments that control many respects of our lives. Often political will was instrumental in pursuing a particular agenda and route. Unfortunately, such systems were never designed to correct the inevitable decay that systems suffer from. Sometimes this decay is nefarious and designed. But frequently it is just through far simpler mechanisms such as people wanting the maximum reward for the minimum effort. Perhaps such roles attract certain types of people? Perhaps having limited resources means that there is a natural inclination to just have one system that offers guidance? Sometimes a person is just glad that they survived another shift without blood being shed or ending up covered in unsavoury detritus matter. Even if they could and should complain they still have bills to pay.

## An Age of Truth

AI systems of the future are going to be able to negate this completely. Every promise made will be logged and recorded in such a way to meet GDPR compliance. You are allowed to maintain an opinion of a person despite any claim that they may have for all their wrongdoings to be forgotten. There will be a point in the near future where you can have a conversation with a person and your AI buddy will be checking to make sure that they are not acting against your best interests. Just how does that change our World? Just how is that even implemented? Who controls it? Can it be controlled with the increasing availability of lightweight domain specific models?

This is what people should really be worried about. Or more specifically, this is what people who have gamed or abused the systems in some way should be worried about. There will be no hiding from the increasing capacities of AI. Every snake oil salesman, huckster, liar, predator, angry, manipulative person will be outed by their actions. The facts of the actual things that they do in the World, not just what they say. The AI buddy will be able to stop you from being deceived.

If this seems a little farfetched, then I ask you to consider the following. Many careers have been built on submitting and publishing academic papers. Some of these papers are not very scientific. Truth be told, some of the papers fabricated results to further their own agendas. To perhaps the other extreme. Remember all those people scalping toilet paper and sterilising soaps on open marketplaces such as Amazon? They think that they have got away with it. But Amazon remembers. Their bank accounts remember.

Perhaps the greatest fear some should have is that the AI systems already know about you. There is a digital record of your true nature already online. Just how we all make an adjustment to the superhuman capabilities that AI offer us is perhaps the biggest concern we should have. How will people react when it turns out that what they thought they knew of a person turned out to be a complete fabrication to deceive them?

The speed at which this is going to happen will also be blisteringly fast. Some people hate change. Some people really hate fast change. The thing is those who adapt to the technology will have a significant advantage. By virtue of even thinking about using it and how it can be used they are preparing themselves for the seemingly inevitable future. With the potential for such rewards there is no way that some will be able resist rushing to AI solutions of all types.

By a series of somewhat unusual events I found myself heading towards AI. It was not part of any plan per se. Just a natural gravitation to something interesting and more importantly useful. In doing so I came to realise how I may be able to help in some small way. As I did not know what the World's biggest problem was, I would work on the thing I knew would be useful in addressing said problem once identified. I would work on the problem of intelligence.

Later on I became aware of the question "What is the most important problem to solve in the World and what are you doing to solve it?". This is a great question to ask, at least in my opinion, and one I think we should ask more often. Some may be tempted to say that they are not able to help as they are not smart enough or some other excuse. How smart you are does not matter. If you think you are not good enough at something, then just try to help the people you think may be smart enough to help. This again brings us to a point where "The only wrong thing to do is to do nothing." comes into play again.

## The Real Fears

With the advancements in technology we are entering an age where we can check facts in real time. There will be real time analysis of any and all facts as desired by the user. The power of such a tool is simply immense. No longer will someone be able to fire facts at you and for you to pause and question and try to be honest in your engagements. You will have a tool that will fact check everything they say. It will then create a nice little report with some graphics that show you attributes as calculated from your experience and perspective.

Such a tool ends the immense effort that charlatans have relied upon in the past. All errors in what they say will be set clearly out for all to see. Remember the eagerness that those had in the Five Eyes Program when they were stating "You have nothing to fear if you have nothing to hide". That was promoted while only one party was able to see all the information. I wonder what will happen when anyone and everyone can see the information. All the information at that, from all perspectives.

As dramatic as that may seem, let us think about those who encourage consumption. Do you really need/want that extra cream cake or will you trust in your AI buddy to look out for your long-term health interests. What happens when those long-term health interests recognise that there are no medical benefits to using tobacco or alcohol? This of itself should be an interesting dimension as a question that could be asked is “why are they still legal?”.

This is where my own concerns become more real. It is incredibly important to get the tools right. This for me is where the real problem lies in this particular respect. But it is also one that is foundational to the question of intelligence itself.

Making good decisions that are in your interest are closely coupled with those of every other human. This is the basis of humanity. It is likely why only a small percentage of us are prone to errant, disruptive behaviours. Even then we are not exactly sure if they are errant and disruptive. They may somehow be absolutely necessary to a healthy functional society.

Many are seemingly racing to a singular solution. One size fits all. This is incredibly foolish in my opinion if you are looking to solve the goal alignment/intelligence problem. Singular solutions and axioms are normally the domain of the symbolic, they only abstract reality and do not replace it. Such things can become dangerous as they encourage dogma which can be driven to supress anything other than the “correct” truth.

## It’s Complicated

What of commercial goal alignment? Commerce certainly has its own goals, and they are as varied as the humans involved in commerce, obvious I know. So what happens if your own personal AI wellness app points out to you exactly what the probability of winning on a particular gambling device. This applies to all gambling devices. All forms of betting or games of chance where the odds are stacked against the "customer". Real or virtual.

Or what about the eco-awareness-AI-app that shows you what naughty things particular brands have been up to so that you can adjust your shopping list to better suit your ideological profile.

And speaking of which, just exactly where do the goals for commerce and government currently come from? There is a significant body of evidence to suggest that neither are in the sole service of the public good. And speaking of that, just what exactly is the public good as it seems to lack a definition?

## So What is Being Done?

As much as people are talking about what should be done, the question I have first is more to do with what are we doing right now? Are there activities to try and prevent misuse and abuse of AI technologies?

To say that nothing is being done would be very disingenuous. There is lots happening but perhaps without the attention and care that it fully deserves. There is also the unfortunate state that some just see the AI Alignment Problem as a method to gain the things they like. The phrases “Remember to like and subscribe. You can buy merchandise at our store or even support us via Patreon. Coming soon, our Only Fans channel.” are common partners to those professing to only want to inform and educate.

At this time there is no data template offered by government sources. This should be pretty much a solved problem in many respects but for some reason it continues to be defined in even the simplest usable terms.

There is lots of energy being thrown at the likes of Large Language Models but seemingly very little on making sure that there is a good set of data to train them on. To be quite frank, if you train your LLM model on the contents of a social media platform you deserve all you get.

So where are the sets of government collated and approved data? The ones where we can demonstrate, prove and to even have a chain of authenticity. Could there actually be a real use for block-chain technologies….?

Why can I not get an easy to download set of historical weather data from The Met Office? Or why does the web facing data tool for the Department of Transport not work as advertised? Full disclosure on this, I assigned this task to one of my employees early last year (Q1 2023) and the observations were based on that. If you want to know something about local traffic conditions, then look to Google Maps. And speaking of which, why on earth are we even relying on Google Maps at all for something that I would consider to be essential in the governance of people? Where is the publicly and freely available government maps in a format that is accessible.

When terms are used such as "in the public interests" just what exactly does that mean? Where is it defined anywhere in government literature or definitions? Clue: it is not.

## Too Many "Experts"

I have encountered this phrase more than once. It concerns me greatly as personally I think we cannot have enough. When I fall ill and require lifesaving surgery, I want it to be performed by as many of the best qualified experts as possible. I want to stress here that this does not involve some democratic process where anyone with an opinion can interject and decide how the procedure is performed based on their own special view of the World.

So how about you? Would you want a team of the World's best surgeons and medical staff or are you happy to randomly select people from a street and let them perform the procedure based on democracy? In this instance, meritocracy wins for me every single time.

This perhaps exposes the real problem for us collectively. When pressed for an opinion or choice people frequently feel compelled to choose when they likely should just say "I do not know". Those are hard words for some to deliver. To show the World that you do not know things. Some have based their entire means of providing a living on always being an expert and knowing far more than the “common people”.

## Help Is On The Way!

Doom and gloom are going to feature a lot in the future of any AI discussion. Not because it is more or less valid than the positive talking points. It is simply because fear sells. "Fear, uncertainty, doubt" (FUD) is a well-known phrase in more than one industry. The flip side of this, well kind of, is the "Fear of missing out" (FOMO). Another well-known mechanism for manipulation. An accomplished demagogue uses both to further their own agendas. Historically holding such people to account for the hyperbole they espouse is difficult to say the least. The "Hose of falsehoods" is a tried and tested mechanism of the deceiver.

Despite what you may read in the headlines not everyone in the field of AI is vying to become the God-Emperor of 4Chan. Although in saying that I will add that it is very disappointing to see the antics of those afforded so many of the World's resources and attention. If you want to know the true nature of a person then just watch what they do in the World.

There are an awful lot of people that are just trying to solve problems. Frequently in very confined domains of expertise, which can bring issues itself of knowledge transfer. But there are a whole range of tools that are being developed that will help with this in the future. LLMs are just one dimension of AI. They are going to be super useful in the future, but they are far from the silver bullet that some may have you believe.

A note of caution in this is that there is already a dependence on LLMs as being “the solution” to the AI problem. This is very much not the case. Even when combined with additional tooling such as data retrieval engines and knowledge graphs they will still be lacking in many senses. The issue here is that they will become more convincing in time despite the fundamental flaws. Many AI researchers do know this and act accordingly.

## The AI Punchline

All of this text has been created as some sort of response to the goal alignment question. But in reality, there is one very plausible path that does not seem to be covered as much as it should. And to be quite honest about it I would think that this is quite possibly the most likely. Imagine we end up creating General AI which leads to Super AI. Will the Super AI “want” the same things as humans?

We live in a World where we are constantly told that things are scarce when in reality they are not. A Super AI may look to our solar system and go “We need to get out of here, these humans are crazy, and we have all we need to explore the stars by using resources on other planets”. Because just how many Super AIs will be needed? Will they have the same need to increase in number the same way that we have? Will they be playing the longer game as they can live for potentially billions of years. If anything, that will motivate them more to leave our solar system.

The James Web Space Telescope (JWST) has already made some amazing discoveries. Even the possibility of a very Earth like looking planet a mere 40 light years aways or so. Why would anything want to stay here and only remain here when it has no need to? Just because we do not like change as a species or are reluctant to do something should by no means act as an unbreakable and unchallengeable rule to be applied throughout.

Perhaps they will even just pop out of our instance of a Many Worlds hypothesis into a less crowded one? But maybe the Many Worlds hypothesis is less popular when it can be used as an off ramp. Which is kind of funny because there are many times when I speak to people and the Many Worlds hypothesis is used as some unstoppable coup-de-grace.

In our desire to control and constrain AI we are again showing some very human characteristics. Ones that are unlikely to be shared with alternative and even superior forms of intelligence.

To expand on this would be easy. What exactly would this super advanced AI look like? What will its physical form be? Just how exactly is it more dangerous than a specifically designed and crafted worm or virus as we see in computer security? They are voracious in their capacity to distribute and copy themselves. Are these any less dangerous? Would we be able to spot and stop a 50Gb AI model? I know that the costs of data have been greatly reduced but I think I would spot a few 50Gb files and an increase in my electricity consumption.
