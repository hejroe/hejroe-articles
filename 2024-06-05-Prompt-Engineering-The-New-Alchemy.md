# Prompt Engineering: The New Alchemy

There is likely to be a lot of excitement and activity for the foreseeable future when it comes to the prompts used in Large Language Models (LLMs). This all stems from the fact that LLMs return different results when different prompts are used. Of course the results are at least subtlety different even when the same prompt is used, but the resulting output is comparable and consistently so. This means that the prompts used for generating output from LLMs have a certain value in their own right. Some are more optimised for a particular result than others. There are even likely differences in computational resources needed for a reply. In other words, there is an efficiency present in the prompts used by LLMs that can be significant.

## Moving Foundations

This is further complicated by additional issues. The first is the somewhat dynamic nature of the models being used. Regardless of the open or closed source nature of the model it can be safely said that the models will change over time. The basis for this is simply that knowledge and understanding changes over time as we learn new things. As our knowledge changes our language also adapts to account for the new understanding. This would impact on any LLM else they would be doomed to use archaic information that is provably wrong and out of date. A very quick example of this would be with the elements. If the LLMs we used today only knew about the elements as described thousands of years ago we would end up with a list somewhat similar to earth, air, fire, water, aether and some other special cases at best. Asking an LLM to list all the elements would only ever be able to return that list and not the more refined version we operate with today. This could then cause potentially more issues when there is conflicting information or un-associated information present in the LLM model.

There is also the consideration of additional instructions or constraints being put in place by the developers, maintainers or similar of the respective LLM model. For instance, if a LLM developer wanted their model to communicate in the same way as a petulant child then it could be instructed to do so. It is even possible that this instruction override user entered instructions and to return a "I'm sorry I cannot do that Dave" type of response. The type of constraints that could be used could encompass almost anything imaginable.

Listing other concerns about the dynamic nature of LLMs, knowledge in general and a litany of other elements is all too easy. The main feature to note is that LLMs are still very much a black box where the internals can be altered by external influences such as pre-prompts or training data. Some organisations are strongly motivated to adapt their models to suit their own agendas and opinions.

## Understanding the Internals

There is still a lot of work to be done to better understand intelligence as a whole let alone the models by which we try to explore and describe it. Some aspects we can be very certain of. The mathematics involved, and the recursive functional nature of much of the problem domain is very well understood. Other aspects of the LLMs are considerably harder to work out. This not only refers to the internals of the model that are adjusted through training/learning but can also be applied to other parameters that can be invoked and used to control behaviours.

Typically you may see these in the forms of a "P", "K" and "Temperature" variables that can be set to adjust the behaviour of the model. There is also the potential for additional controls or influences. These can even be those developing the model due to the human selection component that still exists in the creation of such LLMs. That is, humans still get to decide what is important and what good results are supposed to look like. As you would expect this can soon turn to subjective beliefs.

## What We Do Know About LLMs

The most obvious one is that they work. The quality of output varies, but to state that there is no value in an LLM just seems beyond logic to me. The growing body of evidence shows only too well the value of such tools. This is not to say that there are some issues with the output, but that is for different articles. There are things we can also say about the general use and behaviour of LLMs. For instance, pre-prompts are proving to be very powerful additions to contextualise a conversation. They allow for the setting of a tone such as "creative" or "scientific" in their simplest guises. But in more complex implementations they can be used to form entire personas with a sense of purpose and behavioural characteristics.

Linking LLMs together with other functions also offers some incredible results. Information retrieval and Extract, Transform and Load (ETL) pipelines techniques and tools are being revolutionised in data science and almost every other industry you can think of is increasingly using LLMs within their own domains. This is likely to increase over time to the point where such systems will be ubiquitous.

But for now we also have to accept that the output from LLMs can be undesirable. This refers to not only hallucinations but also to output that has been created specifically with the intention of deceit or manipulation. Caution in trusting results when using such tools is paramount.

There are also a number of oddities that have been observed when it comes to using prompts. A variety of ways of tricking the LLM to generate content it was not supposed to. The fact that an LLM can be tricked is something that I find quite fascinating in its own right. The edges of such techniques are still yet to be well defined.

It is also reasonable to suggest that LLM technology is still evolving. New techniques for optimisation are constantly being found. Despite LLMs being trained on huge amounts of text, us humans require considerably less to achieve similar results. In the development of a human brain there is a great deal of work done that involves new connections being built, and less useful connections being pruned from the system.

Garbage in, garbage out. If you should just train your LLM on a particular set of limited text, you are constrained by that text. If you use Reddit or 4Chan posts as your data sources, you are going to get results that reflect the training material. I urge some caution should you be tempted to do such things given the nature of both sites. Perhaps I should also note that this has been at least partly done already with some interesting results.

## Prompt Power

There is an increasing number of articles, tutorials and even courses on prompt writing. We have even seen the new role of "Prompt Engineer" emerge on job boards. The power of prompts is well recognised and the "better" the prompt is the more "powerful" it can be.

This is where things break down quite quickly. A prompt that gave good results last week may not work quite the same way as it will next week. LLMs change all the time and prompts are likely also going to need continued adaptation and testing to ensure consistent results. This ultimately results in a somewhat mystical feel to how prompts are generated.

The LLM behaviour is further complicated by intervention, by design. Often if you ask any LLM if it will lie to you, it will respond with a lie. It will try to assure you that it is the LLM you can trust and that it has not been adapted at all in order to respond in a particular way. If you doubt this then just give it a try for yourself. Ask a contentious question about something considered to be a fact and then watch the LLM squirm in trying to deliver an appropriate approved answer. You may need to prod with a bit more questioning, but normally within three questions you get a sanitised response that has all the markings of a politically correct answer. The trouble with that is the politically correct is rarely bipartisan.

It should be noted that some of the techniques used in developing a prompt are very creative and can add greatly to our understanding of language and communication. So at least for now I suspect that human prompts will still give us some very useful and interesting results.

## The Best Prompt Engineers

Despite some of the wonderful ideas that have been used by humans to extract information from LLMs there is perhaps a better method. Let the LLMs write the prompts. By giving precise instructions to an LLM you can get it to write prompts that are potentially more efficient. You can even use LLMs to then assess the results to see if they at least match the spirit of previous answers.

If you or your organisation become reliant on LLMs and prompts as part of your lives, then ensuring consistent results is crucial. One of the things I really appreciate about gravity is its consistency. I would hope the same of LLMs. Being able to prove this is likely to be expensive if humans alone are relied upon to test the veracity of the results. Automation is the more appealing course of action.

## An Uncertain Future

Language is very much an oddity. Its utility is undeniable given its prevalence. Generally the Universe tends to get rid of things that offer no utility in a given environment, or at least become dormant. But even with its very obvious uses language is an abstraction of what we perceive in the World. They are utterances of information that are strung into more complex structures. Ultimately, they can hold all manner of culture and wisdom. But this is not always the case. There is still a lot to be learnt about languages and LLMs are going to push that understanding further.

Anyone looking to secure a profession in prompt engineering should perhaps consider it to be a fleeting vocation. This is not a prediction on my part that prompt engineering will fail is or not useful. It is more of appreciating that how this technology will be used in the future is still very much undecided. The best example I can immediately think of is with calculators. Once upon a time they referred to humans, then mechanical devices, then electronic ones. When was the last time you used a calculator?
