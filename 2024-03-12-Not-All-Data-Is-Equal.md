# Not All Data Is Equal

There are many aspects of our modern world that exist solely within the digital realm. A great deal of music, art, literature and more besides can find itself residing in a digital format exclusively. As with other forms of history we rely on the artifacts of previous generations to form an understanding of the past. But what of the digital ages?

In modern times we use books, literature, the arts and more to record what has been done in the past. Often there is a lot of guesswork and error involved. This is especially more so in the case of societies that were not reliant on literacy. There have been many societies that formed on the basis of knowledge being transferred in stories, song, and dance.

Even in an age when you would think that everything will be recorded forever, it is in reality very much not the case. It would be reasonable to say that it is not only impossible but also undesirable. The impossibility simply comes from how much "memory space" would be required to store all data that happens and to do so indefinitely. In a comparatively short period of time all of the available matter we have would be used on creating data storage of one kind or another.

A more relatable explanation would be with how we seem to store data from our own experiences. We do not store everything ever, despite the claims of some. When you start to think about just how much information our bodies are exposed too it is simply immense. There are mechanisms present that trim our experienced data/information into more practical abstractions. But in this process a lot of detail is lost through compression. For instance, if you were to think of a scene with lots of detail from your past you would create the minute details as you think they should appear. This would be especially evident if the scene you remembered had a tree with lots of leaves or a field of grass. Each leaf or blade of grass is not remembered as an individual component, but more as a method by which we understand leaf or grass blade behaviour. So when we remember the tree or field, we build a more complete representation from more primitive components.

If further evidence is needed, then just think of all the data being streamed at you by the Universe. Can you really store all that information at every moment in time? Again, it is simply a physical impossibility to do so using the computing techniques we currently have available. And this is unlikely to ever change, unless we stumble across something quite unexpected.

Ultimately this means that some information gets left behind. This then raises the questions on what data is retained and what is lost. What mechanisms exist to handle the compression of experienced information from our senses?

## Knowledge in Language

As obvious as this is, knowledge can be held in language. In this we can make some interesting observations. Language is a method by which information can be exchanged. It is complex and nuanced and covers a number of different forms. Language and communication can be conveyed by speech, art, music, literature and more. There are even specialised symbolic languages that exist exclusively to describe knowledge as with mathematics or music.

Interestingly, language to me can also been seen as a map of utility. Words that are used all the time are useful and hold something that is valued somehow. When these are strung together, they can form concepts and ideas. Words and sentences that have utility are preserved through use, those that do not will be consigned to history in some form or another. As new things are created new language is also created to account for them. The verbs "tweet" or "google" have come into contemporary use with the advent of the internet. Prior to which their respective use was considerably less and more prominent in a different specific domain.

When you examine the way a language changes over time you can also see that it can be used to create an understanding of a certain point in time. The words used and how often they appear can tell us all manner of things about a society. At one point people would only write about horses, then chariots, then carriages or wagons, then cars. As each word was replaced its use was reduced. When all of these changes are sequenced, you can even see the dynamic morphing of a language. You can even see the interaction between neighbouring languages which can then offer all manner of insights into human migration for instance.

The distribution of spoken languages is an interesting subject. Some factors that seem important are the number of people who speak the language and the knowledge held within the language. Diversity also seems to play an important role in determining the knowledge seemingly stored within a language. Although Mandarin is the most spoken by native population, English is the most spoken. The number of countries with an official language of English is considerably more than Mandarin and also English has a far greater geographical diversity. Historically English has also proven to be central in the distribution of knowledge. Hungarian however is likely to contain less knowledge than either Mandarin or English due to only being spoken by about 13 million people. This is not to slight Hungarian and is by no means a certainty. Just probabilistically the case. No matter the order of what languages have more or less knowledge is somewhat moot, we just need to appreciate that there will be some hierarchical order.

So if we were to train a Large Language Model using the most and least used languages, which one do you think will contain more knowledge? Probabilistically the most used language will contain more knowledge. This is not an absolute given of course as a less used language may hold the answer to the meaning of life for instance. But the chances of this happening are slim and even then, any such knowledge would soon be translated to more used languages. The thing about languages is that they are very mercenary when it comes to culling old words to make room for the new ones. Even entire meanings are flipped on their heads where the negative becomes the positive and vice versa. So if a new concept/idea comes along that offers a lot of utility then you can expect it to travel very quickly into all languages.

## Salient Information Extraction

The processes that are present that enable us to quickly observe, process and use our experienced data are far from being completely understood. In other organisms we can see hints of more efficient processes that are possible. Our ability to memorise a sequence of numbers is eclipsed by that of other primates, namely the chimpanzee. This means that whatever it is that humans do is at some point different to that of the mechanisms used by our distant cousins. They may come from the same or similar place, but the difference in results is quite marked.

It is possible that the way humans process such a task has more steps in it that causes latency. Alternatively it may be entirely different mechanisms that have come about independently of each other. One being far more efficient than the other. Irrespective, the difference in performance suggests that there is no single solution to the "numbers in a sequence" problem but at least two, and very likely many more.

This also supports the idea of different mental processes happening in some way. These can even be exclusive to different types of people. For instance, different people will value different things in different ways. Another incredibly obvious statement I know. But this can then make all the difference between something happening or not. If someone does not determine the value of a thing to be above a threshold of some sort, then it will likely be forgotten. No value, then no point in storing the information.

How the value of data is determined appears to be quite a complex thing. Even in all the human examples there are spectrums of function. An evaluation technique for one person can be different or absent altogether for another. The fact that there is this difference is especially interesting as it hints at something more. You see, an individual's knowledge is a subset of all knowledge. This then means that the individual humans each represent a small slice of the entire knowledge of humanity.

The main point here perhaps is simply that there are different mechanisms for evaluating the value of data being observed and retained.

## Compressing the Universe

At first this may seem like a tall order. But again, once you apply a bit of thought you soon realise that compressing all the information in the Universe is something that you do all the time. At every moment you discard huge amounts of data and reduce aspects of the World you consider important into more manageable abstractions.

Compression can be perfect where every detail can be restored with complete accuracy. It can also be a brutal affair where a great deal of information is simply lost. The tension between lossy and lossless compression is one ever present in commercial/transactional settings but in humans it seems to be a stranger affair. How us humans receive, store, and retrieve information is far from  a solved problem.

One of the more interesting observations that can be made in retention of information is that it is a range scale for people. Different people not only remember different things but also for different periods of time. Some seem able to remember events with perfect clarity from decades ago. Whereas others struggle to recall events from a few hours ago or less. This again seems to be one of those areas where we have to think of humans in both the individual and collective senses. I should even mention "memory portals", also known as doors. They seem to be a very effective method of wiping information from a brain.

This can be further complicated by how humans seem to even process data. Some have an internal monologue and are able to visualise sights, sounds and other sensory information, while some people do not seem to have that same capacity. This creates huge ranges of diversity and complexity even though the observed differences are seemingly negligible from moment to moment.

## Where is the Value?

Perhaps such a simple concept as value is key in answering questions about how salient data is extracted, retained, and retrieved. Yet again this is different for different people. It can even be different from different times. For instance on a hot day I will value an ice-cream differently to a cold and wet day. However, my children seem to value ice-cream more consistently than myself.

Again we observe differences that hint at different implementations of similar functions. Somewhere within us is a function that determines the perceived value of a thing. If you were to see a sequence of 12 numbers, you would interpret the importance based on other factors. Perhaps the numbers are just randomly generated, or a telephone number. Better still the winning lottery numbers. The information would be the same for any participating observer, but the value instilled could vary wildly.

As if all of this were not enough, there are further perceived complexities. If you were talk to a behavioural biologist and/or neuroscientist you would encounter concepts of where low-level biological systems within us influence greatly our behaviours. Then we have the realms of conscious and subconscious decision making.

One of the things that I think about at times is the deterministic nature of our lives. Is our life already predetermined, as some have boldly claimed as an undeniable fact? Or is it something that we determine for ourselves? There are of course many instances where the choices available to us seem to support the idea that our behaviours are predetermined. But the choices presented to us may not be as clear cut or complete as we think they might be. This is worth keeping in mind as some of the perceived complexity of data collection and processing can be hallucinations. But these are considered so credible for whatever reason that they are accepted as a truth. In reality it is possible that far simpler mechanisms can accurately describe the human data processes. It would also be perhaps a tad foolish to not consider the opposite also being true where seeming simplicity is actually a complex dynamic system, just perhaps observed from a different level of scale. With this I hope to also convey the possibility that any perceived value is very much a construction of our own minds in order to justify the actions and feelings we can have.
