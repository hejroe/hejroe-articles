# DANGER: AI Art

Last year I expected legal concerns to become more of a thing in relation to AI generated material, in all its forms. I was quite wrong about that, but only in the sense of timing. My prediction, along with many others, is becoming more of a reality.

Smaller companies have had a boon of late with a newfound ability to generate artistic output of all types, written, visual, auditory and more. But these happy times are coming to an end as pathways are being constructed in legal landscapes. The consequences can vary greatly from region to region and intent can play a part in establishing foul play. But let's not dwell on such issues. Most companies just want to do lawful business and have no intention of "stealing IP" from others. So, let's focus on ensuring your artistic output is unlikely to infringe on anyone's rights.

## A Blast from the Past

This is more of two blasts from the past. By this I am referring to a couple of articles already published. The first is “The Great LLM Detection Tools Rip-off” and the second is the "AI Explanation for Artisans". I will try to condense the actual point in relation to this piece here rather than forcing you to look, as I suspect you will not (I know I would not).

The detection tools piece was about the fact that some content is inevitably going to look the same. I made this point using a "Hello World" program. A very simple program that prints out "Hello World" that is very common to encounter when learning a new programming language. More commonly you could use a "stick figure" of a person. With a stick figure you just have lines for limbs, torso, neck and a circle for a head. If you were to ask a thousand people to draw a stick figure, then you would get clusters of ones that are very similar. In such instances it should be apparent that proving someone infringed on someone else's rights becomes more difficult. Although I think it is also important to note that the legal systems of some places are more than accommodating to the "right" sort of person.

The second part may be a bit trickier to convey. I will try my best to condense this idea as compactly as I can. Even though a great deal of art is used to train a model there are still spaces of unique content. For instance, think of that numbers one and two as representing a different piece of art. 1 can equal a minimalist style and 2 represents cubism. The whole numbers in this instance represent a different piece/style but then there is the infinite number of variations in between. All those 0.0001 differences have a meaning, just as do the 0.000001 differences. So, all the numbers between 1 and 2 hold all the different versions of minimalism and cubism. The boundaries are set between 1 and 2 and all the variations live in between. To push this analogy further, the idea of 0 and 3 has not even been discovered yet.

Looking at both pieces from a slightly higher perspective/abstraction you could say that one is dealing with the ordinary/un-original and the other the unique/original. And these ideas are on what could be described as a scale. In other words, at some point the ordinary becomes unique, and quite where that is can be very subjective.

## Framing the Issue

What this means in more practical terms is that some care is required when generating AI art of any form. For example, imagine you have a tyre replacing service and your surname is Mann. A natural thing to do with a Large Language Model (LLM) is to perhaps create a man made of tyres? Now just what sort of response do you think you will get? Will it be an original piece of work or will it look like the Michelin Man, iconic symbol of a manufacturer? What about Mario and their plumbing services company?

I should stress here that I am extending the term LLM to include both image and audio generation. This is mainly due to the line between such systems is disappearing fast. Already there are models that can do text, image, video and audio work in both comprehension (as much as LLMs comprehend of course) and generation.

In the example offered with the tyre and plumbing service I hope to convey the risks in generating content. It is perfectly reasonable to think of a situation where someone has never heard of the Michelin Man or of Mario the plumber from the iconic game series. If they are to use output generated by an LLM that resembles, or is exactly like, the intellectual property of someone else then what happens? Who is at fault here? Is it the LLM developer, the LLM user, the writer of the prompt being used, the very notion of intellectual property? It can all get quite confusing pretty quickly.

## Actions not Words

Looking at the actions of the developers of the models is very useful. Frequently what people say is used to hide what they actually do. To cut a long story short, the indemnification offered by LLM developers is lacking and is normally reserved for paying customers. This idea can be extended further. How many LLM developers are happy for their training data and methods to be public? I would argue not many, not even the ones proclaiming to be "for the people". Now I am sure that there are many reasons for this that can be conjured up, but the fact often remains. LLM developers are secretive about the data that they have used.

With the bar being lowered so much for generating content you can expect a lot of content to be generated. Anyone with even the slightest notion of creativity (or laziness) can use a tool to generate a thing that was previously more prohibitive to do. This then creates a lot of output that looks and feels very familiar. This is where the owners of intellectual property find themselves having to act, else they lose their claim.

Another interesting question to ask is how the individual business owner may seek to protect themselves. It is common to be able to be covered by someone for almost any risk. Quite how much the premiums are is another matter. But commercial general liability insurance is available, for a price.

Litigation and threats of it are increasing against model providers and users. There is even a bizarre situation of different LLM developers making claims of foul play performed by their competitors. This is made all the more amusing by said accusers also not releasing the same information.

A final note is the terms and conditions offered by the LLM providers. In most cases they are not responsible for any output in any way whatsoever. Additionally, if you happen to be on an internet LLM service then there is the potential for the providers to even learn from your own input. This would be fine if you could be certain that a user never enters in copyrighted material. But surely that would never happen (this is sarcasm just to be perfectly clear).

## Stop Using AI Art?

Here we can invoke Betteridge’s Law of Headlines, no! It is necessary for you to be more cautious in how you approach such a matter. A general rule I have always found useful in life is "how will this sound in a court of law or public opinion?". Despite the dramatic framing the intent here is to force you to reflect on what your actions were. Did you do the things expected of a normal level of due diligence for instance? Did you try to prevent harm and were your intentions well intended? Did you keep a record of the interaction which generated the content in the first place?

The last point in the list is something that is available in a way from LLM providers as you can often capture/download a work session, with all the thinking included in some instances. But how you record and retain that information as some form of defence is a problem you are left with. Most systems currently simply do not cater to the needs of real businesses, and they are still wrapped up in some tech-bro futurist arms race or whatever it is being framed as today.

## Being Practical

The reality is that being able to generate new content in any form enables a company to do things that were previously out of their reach. This I think is a good thing to do. I also think that the following little list may go some way to protect you from a bad ruling should you ever find yourself in a legal spotlight for infringing intellectual property of others.

Do it yourself. Yes, I know this sounds counter intuitive, so an explanation is required. AI is going to save a lot of time. Just what are you going to do when you have so much spare time on your hands? I would suggest you get a skill. You can even be really bad at it as you just need enough originality to act as a seed for an LLM. Take art for instance. You cannot draw at all, and you only have MS Paint available. But this is enough to create a unique starting point for your LLM generated art. You can tell an LLM (of certain types) to take a picture, the one you created with paint, and use that as the starting point for the artistic adventure. Even if it is incredibly bad it still holds enough uniqueness to at least initially reduce the likelihood of you generating art that can be seen to be infringing.

Build your own LLM. Or perhaps even just use one of the open-source ones and fine-tune it as required. You can even skip the fine-tuning if desired. This is a good choice, but you are reliant on the claims of the LLM provider still unless you test it for yourself.
Use more than one model. This involves generating multiple images with different models. These can then be fused/combined into a single image. This reduces risk again but does not eliminate it. Also, there is a "common understanding" that is likely to be encountered. I also use the "wisdom of crowds" here as it relates to a general understanding of a concept or thing. If every model contains the same data, then they will likely still generate output that creates the potential for liability. But the point here is that you actively tried to avoid infringement.
Do an image/text/audio reverse search. Or if possible, even to search for your own generated ones and find similar matches. If nothing is coming back, or if what comes back looks nothing like your own, then you are probably in a good spot.
Keep a record of how material is generated. Just quite how you do this in a provable chain that is robust and has audit capabilities is another matter. There are solutions that can help with this that are being developed. But that is something for another day.
The final entrant in this listing is to simply place the risk somewhere else. Just use a service that allows you to request art from someone else. Then the liability is theirs as you were simply paying for a service. In other words, use the model that already exists.

## Act Now

This is one of those areas where you need to act sooner or later. You may well think "I am too small working in a small geographical area, they will never spot me", and that will be very wrong. In the future, and I could easily say right now, there will be a plethora of tools that will allow people to track all intellectual property from everywhere all the time. Go back 900 hundred years and ask someone what the weather is like today in Africa and they would likely go with "What is Africa?". The point here is that they had no concept of a thing and simply did not care about it as the information was not there. Nowadays a quick search and you can see almost real time (latency precision here for any nerds reading) the weather as it happens. This is a trend we should expect to continue.
