# 2025 - A Year of AI in Review

At the start of this year I wrote a piece that tried to anticipate the major movements in the field of AI. Then at the end of the year I write another piece that looks at what I got right, or more importantly what I got wrong. This is with the intention of simply trying to strive to be better.

In the piece from the start of the year I made a number of predictions. None of them were outlandish ones really as I am generally quite conservative and restrained in my thoughts and opinions. At least I am when it comes to matters of prediction.

But surprisingly this year I was not actually wrong on any of the points made. I think I even managed to get most of the more significant trends. Thankfully I did not claim to be so bold as to say who would do what or when. This would have only resulted in error. I mean no mainstream pundits at the start of the year would have predicted the interesting Hierarchical Recursive Model as detailed by Samsung. There were also many other interesting developments in that regard, such as Deepseek.

There were many other advancements made and truths confronted in the realm of AI research. I even published a couple of pre-prints myself, as I lack academic connections or even will. AI also affected many other fields of scientific endeavour. Far too many to mention here. These were often overlooked though as they were not as alluring as headlines of data centre projects and multi-billion investment deals.

I have even seen a shift this year in those naysayers that rejected AI tools as rubbish. They are coming around to the utility of such mechanisms. Are the tools perfect? Not at all. But being able to spot AI generated material is becoming increasingly harder to do. This is impressive in its own right given how entrenched they were in despising all things AI.

Perhaps the area where I was wrong is that when I originally wrote the piece I was expecting it to take a full year for all of the predictions to come to some sort of reality. However by March it seemed that all that I had predicted had already become true.

This left me in the unusual position of expecting to be able to eat humble pie in this piece and yet there is no pie to consume.

In this I want to make it clear at just what an incredible year it has been for advancements. There has of course been lots of talk about AI wars and bubbles, but this just seems normal business to me. Private companies, markets and media tend to use such language irrespective of how it aligns with the real world. Are certain companies and technologies over valued? Yes. Is scaling LLMs on more data going to result in AGI? No. Is there a moat? No, well not as much as larger organisations would like us to believe.

With so much advancement in 2025 I am truly excited as to what 2026 will bring. Of course I have written a predictive piece that you can also find on this site.
