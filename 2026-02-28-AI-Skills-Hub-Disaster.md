# UK's AI Skills Hub Disaster

I live and breathe AI. It is very much almost all that I do in my waking hours. Even when unable to work on it directly, my free thoughts will go back to considering it in one way or another. I would argue that this is not the religious AI fervour of a zealot, but a pragmatic and well-founded view formed from first principles. I say this as I am hoping to gain some credibility with you for the point(s) I am trying to make later. This is because in this article I would be most grateful if you test my word. Put in that bit of effort to verify what I write. Nothing outrageous is required in the way of effort. Just a simple "is this article nonsense and what is wrong with it?" dropped into your preferred LLM will be more than ample for the task. I will happily confess to doing this myself with a variety of LLMs when I review my own work prior to publishing. The normal rebuke I get is that I meander a lot. I can live with that as I quite like meandering.

In my previous article, I tried to encourage others to "Build It Now." The tools now available are simply incredible and getting better by the day. With this new tooling comes a new set of workflows with the potential to greatly impact productivity and costs in ways that were unthinkable just a few years ago. How well these tools are adopted into society is going to be crucial.

Education is essential in this, and I have been personally concerned by the lack of good AI teaching materials. I would argue that actually getting an LLM to teach you is perhaps the best way, provided you think about your prompts carefully. AI does still not seem to be a part of the general education curriculum, yet students are being encouraged down career paths that simply do not, or will not, exist in the very near future. This, to my mind, is an unforgivable cruelty that has to be addressed.

So, I was heartened to see the AI Skills Hub initiative by the UK Government.

Given the massive influence the UK has played in the formation of these cutting-edge technologies, I expected a glimpse of how education could look in the future. I was excited. I signed up. I read things. I used the filtering features. In doing so, it was plainly obvious that the site was less than what I had hoped for. Even less than what I had feared.

It screamed of a total lack of professionalism, understanding of education, and understanding of AI. All were glaringly omitted from a site that was supposed to promote such virtues.

### The "Ghost Town" Experience

To demonstrate quite how bad it is, I will recount my own personal experience.

I made an account and logged in to look for a course or two. I applied filters and ended up selecting "The Alan Turing Institute - Engaging Stakeholders in AI." It seemed like something that should have been read by more of those who created this affront to UK invention.

The page on the AI Skills Hub was simply a brief description of the course and a link to The Alan Turing Institute, where I had to sign up again to take the course. Back on the AI Skills Hub website, I pressed the button to start this course. It immediately allowed me to press another button to complete the course.

So I pressed it. That was it. Course Accomplished!

I hadn't watched a video. I hadn't read a PDF. I had simply clicked two buttons. I selected another free course to make sure I hadn't just found the "black swan" of the catalogue. Nope, that was just the same. Course Accomplished!

I tested this on paid courses too. It seems they have allowed the same approach. Course Accomplished!

This is not a functional learning platform. It is a façade.

### The Crime of Missed Opportunity

The reason I am so disappointed is that to actually have built something far better is incredibly easy given that previous methods and constraints of website and software development no longer apply. Even if I were to take the intent of the current site and rewrite it alone, I could do far better.

For a start, I would ensure that the website complied with UK Government Digital Service (GDS) standards—guidance which is considered among the best in the world. The site I was presented failed to meet these basic accessibility and usability requirements.

But more importantly, PwC (the consultancy behind the site) really should have tried to use AI in a meaningful way in the design process. By asking the AI fundamental questions about the build:

> "What content is needed?"
> "How are we to present the content?"
> "How do we track user progress?"
> "Given the changes to AI and software development how do we future-proof this learning hub?"

My previous article spoke on how programming had changed so much that it no longer looks like programming; it looks a lot more like natural language. This is the content that needs to be presented. Agents, LLMs, and IDEs are going to become invisible. Yet, the content presented on the AI Skills Hub largely consists of funnels into platforms belonging to the main US based AI model providers. Such is their dominance on the website they even have their own dedicated filters.

So have a go and try it now for yourself. See if you get a better set of ideas for a learning hub website than what PwC were able to come up with. From my very human brain, I would say the essential content relates to being able to describe a problem and to come up with pragmatic abstractions/descriptions.

This is why I have written historically about AI being an opportunity for us to become more human. We are going to have to have better thinking skills to allow for effective collaboration with AI tools.

### The Corporate Pipeline & Cultural Erosion

In mentioning this, it is useful to be very open about a simple fact: Software vendors do not give away anything for free. When they let students use their software for free, it is with the intent of securing their own future sales pipeline. People prefer to use what they know. "Get them hooked while they are young" is an unsavoury phrase that applies here. This then means that when the younger generations find employment they will invariably ask for the tools that they are familiar with. This is the same in both the private and public sectors.

This leads to a deeper, more insidious problem. If we rely entirely on US tech giants for our education, we risk becoming a shadow of our former selves — a watered-down version willing to accept how we are perceived as defined by others.

This is already happening. LLMs respond, by their natural defaults, with an awful lot of Zs and are less generous in their use of Us. They use US history, US geography, and US laws. They default to "Fair Use" (US law) rather than "Fair Dealing" (UK law).

We saw a glimpse recently of the UK government intending to track people very carefully to ensure that everything was in "good order". Yet the AI Skills Hub is built on a foundation of "trust" with no "verify." But this is an all-or-nothing solution to a nuanced problem. People are different, and we can and should have software that reflects that.

### Where Are the UK LLMs?

Which brings me to the most worrying question: Where are the UK LLMs?

How as a nation can we be certain that when quizzed, an LLM will respond that the Falkland Islands are a part of the UK? What will the Gulf of Mexico (or the Gulf of America, or Greenland) be called next week? Do check me on that also if you wish.

I have written about this before, and I believe it strongly. We need to be actively involved with AI on the cutting edge, not languishing on the scraps thrown to us by other nation states.

We can do this. We can have a conversation with an LLM right now, asking it: "What are the best standards and practices?" "Has this problem been solved before?" "Can you list the best top 10 educational websites and state why they are the best?"

We do not need the weak "solution" offered by AI Skills Hub. We collectively know what we need and we need to build it now, for ourselves. We should even give ourselves bonus points if we are able to export it without selling it out of the UK altogether.

### Decidedly Disappointed

I have waited a few weeks in the hopes that the UK AI Skills Hub would see some improvements. Alas this was not the case. Even now I still find myself ranting about this as it feels such a disservice to our current and future generations. The UK founded many of the technologies that the World has come to rely on. But we seem to have casually handed over numerous prizes for other nation states to reap the rewards. If the UK continues on this track then we can all expect to use a lot more Zs and less Us in the future. That will be our loss and something we would be unlikely to restore.

There is little doubt in my mind that when I review this piece using a variety of LLMs that they will tell me I am meandering a lot. I agree. This feels a very disjointed piece. No doubt the reason is simply a case of me having far to much I want to say. But I also need to keep on working on solutions that actually work rather than the performative nature of what has been delivered with the UK's AI Skills Hub.
