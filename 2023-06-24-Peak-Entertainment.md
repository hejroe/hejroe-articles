# Peak Entertainment

It is now perfectly possible to think of methods that would allow for every reasonable game of chess to be mapped out. Despite the immense number of possibilities there are many games that are simply not viable, unless you are a pigeon.

This means that there is a number, still a very large one, of all the more interesting games that could be played. This gives a number that we can then use to extrapolate how much time could be taken in watching these different games play out. There is already a great deal of data regarding this and we can even account for the varying forms that the games can take. For instance, we have speed chess, tournament chess of even boxing chess. 

A seventy-year life span contains a total of 613,620 hours. If we simply ignore any complicated issues such as sleep or biological development, then we could say that a person can only watch 613,620 games of chess if each one takes an hour to complete on average. Of course, we can adjust these numbers as we see fit. We could speed up games so that no single game would take more than a minute, this would give us 36,792,000 possible games we could observe in the same time. 

There are dimensions to the enjoyment of spectating in chess that are not being accounted for. Who is playing who, the stakes involved, the potential of rivalries, commentary and analysis and more besides. These characteristics are likely just as important as the moves being played out. It would also not be unreasonable to expect diminishing returns. Would the 10 millionth game of chess be as exciting as the hundredth? The answer to this is of course subjective. 

Now what if you could have any of these games played by any people or things you can think of from any point in time either real or imaginary? If you were a chess super fan this could be considered peak entertainment. To be able to consume games of chess in any way you wished with no repetition, unless asked for, for your entire life.

## Something Other than Chess 

In the example I just use the domain of chess. This could easily be replaced with a myriad of other things that we use to pass time. Any sport or artistic endeavour that you can think of. 

As fanciful as this scenario may seem it is becoming increasingly likely of coming into being. Already there are techniques being developed that allow for 3-dimensional scenes to be created where animated actors/models can be tasked with any number of directives. If you wanted Gollum to be the leading lady in the iconic rom-com Pretty Woman then you could with but a simple prompt. Would I watch it with you? Only for the first few minutes before I head off to watch Howard Moon in an episode of Star Trek. 

## Proof, if it were Needed 

I am sure that some would say that this is nonsense that is unlikely to happen any time soon. To which I respond with a rap battle between the "Fine-Structure" and "Planck" constants (courtesy of Google Bard): 

Fine-Structure Constant: 
I'm the fine-structure constant, I'm here to say, 
I'm the most important number in the universe today. 
I control the strength of the electromagnetic force, 
Without me, there would be no light, no matter, no force. 

Planck Constant: 
I'm the Planck constant, I'm the smallest thing in town, 
I'm the building block of all things, from the smallest quark to the largest crown. 
I'm the foundation of quantum mechanics, 
Without me, there would be no uncertainty, no wave-particle duality, no fun. 

Fine-Structure Constant: 
I may be small, but I'm mighty, 
I'm the key to understanding the universe, 
So step aside, Planck constant, 
I'm the one in charge here. 

Planck Constant: 
Don't underestimate me, fine-structure constant, 
I'm the foundation of everything, 
So don't get too big for your britches, 
I'm the one who's really in control. 

Fine-Structure Constant: 
We'll see about that, Planck constant, 
We'll see who's really in charge here. 

The crowd goes wild as the two constants continue to rap, each one trying to prove their superiority. The battle rages on for hours, with neither constant able to gain an advantage. Finally, the two constants decide to call a truce. They agree that they are both important, and that they are both needed to understand the universe. 

The crowd applauds as the two constants shake hands. They have learned that even though they are different, they can still work together to create a beautiful and complex universe. 

I will happily admit that this is not the greatest of creative works. But it is something I never thought I would ever see written. Is it entertaining? For me it was, at least for a few seconds of its creation.
