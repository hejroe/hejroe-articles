# Looking Ahead for 2026

Last year I described 2025 as being a year of truth. In this I was referring mainly to the fact that tools were becoming more readily available to be able to determine truth, your truth. This meant that those willing to invest in learning and then apply that knowledge were in a position to better gauge all manner of things. What this meant for me is that I had a tool where I could drop in a contract and check to see if there was anything present that would cause me issues or an overreach by one of the parties involved. This is only one aspect of course and there are many others. Any time I was presented with something I had a plethora of tools to help me establish its credibility. If I saw a news headline I could run it past some LLMs and find out more. Was the news being truthful to me or was it doing something else that suited its own agenda? The answer to that is a personal one and I will let you judge for yourself. For me the results were interesting to say the least.

So now I find myself thinking about what 2026 will represent, at least to me. The headline here is trust. A saying I am quite fond of when it comes to trust is that it is gained in drips and lost in buckets. And the matter of trust is ever more important in a world competing for your attention and consumption. Just what news sources do you trust and why? What brands do you trust and why? Who are the people you trust and why? "Trust but Verify" is also another favourite of mine.

## Now what does this mean for me in the realm of AI?

When I start to think about the future of AI I try to keep myself very much focused on what the main players are doing. This does not include what they are saying. Well not to the extent that the speakers of corporate hyperbole may want me to. I tend to look at things that are more reliable indicators of intent.

In 2025 we heard an awful lot about how AI was going to change things and how much money it was going to make. The latter part here is far more interesting. As of writing there seems to be no real recouping of the costs of development. Perhaps more concerning is that there is no clear path as to how that may happen. You see there were promises made about economic savings through AI performing certain tasks but there was very little in the way of tools to actually do this. The issue here is only exposed more by looking at the disclaimers and indemnification that is offered by purveyors of AI solutions. Any protection offered is rather limited in scope.

This leaves a situation of the AI tech leaders talking up their products but not being able to prove it with their bottom line. This is a truth that became more apparent during 2025, and I want to just say Deepseek to show how fragile perceptions can be when it comes to market valuations.

There was a fair amount of talk at the tail end of last year about the relationships and directions of investment into the larger AI companies. Some companies are relatively immune to the costs due to other revenue streams, structure and ownership. Google, Meta and Microsoft for instance. This is only likely temporary. With the significant costs involved in developing and providing AI solutions, this is a folly that can only be supported for a limited time. Generally there is a feeling that a market correction is overdue. This is not to say that AI is not useful, but more that the companies offering such solutions are vastly over valued. I should note that I am fine with all the investment being made as it will accelerate our path towards better AI solutions and even to AGI itself.

Not all the bigger players are worrying in quite the same way. If we were to look at the manufacturers of the hardware and infrastructure then they are having a boon. Nvidia is likely the most well known, but they are not alone. Cloud providers such a Google, Microsoft and Amazon should also be included. There are also a number of lesser prominent names that also have significant success. The likes of Dell or HPE could easily be dropped in. And there are many other companies that are also doing well. I would also note that it is important not to just have a "western" focus. China especially has been very disruptive in 2025 and will continue to be so in 2026.

We have seen AI described as an arms race not only in the sense of different AI developers but also in terms of nation states. East vs. West is a theme that has been developed by pundits and media alike. This has also been picked up at a political/diplomatic level. From my perspective China has been doing very well. Not top of the leaderboards, but that is not as important as some would have you believe. 2025 was a year that China most certainly caught up with their Western rivals. I for one welcome the competition and I use Chinese models often when UK IT infrastructure allows it. I was especially impressed with Qwen having such stability in simple factual and logic questions across languages.

Hardware manufacturers, especially those in chip manufacture are also cashing in. RAM prices have increased significantly and manufacturers are allocating resources to their major chip customers, namely AI developers and providers. This leaves the average consumer in a position of paying considerably more for technology that has not really improved that much over the last few years. This is exacerbated further by uncertainties such as with tariffs or material supply. Quite how this will play out I am less certain of. But at this time I am inclined to think that with customers making harder choices in purchasing decisions that this move by the tech sector could easily backfire. Their big customers insist on having lots of little customers. If you think every person is going to end up paying 200 dollars a month for an LLM then I have a bridge to sell you.

LLMs, for the most part, easily perform the vast majority of tasks that are asked of them with ease. For many that is good enough. And the models that make this happen are freely available and can be easily run on consumer grade hardware. When considering this further you can easily reach a point where you are asking other questions. Just what will the actual market and income streams actually look like? What sectors will exist and how will they be supplied? What technologies to use and what legislation must be followed? There are many more.

The race for AI has also shown that there is a significant difference in the power potential and delivery between different regions. Many are now clamouring to fix and upgrade existing infrastructure to meet the new demands being made of them. This is not cheap or quick. To build a new power facility of any type takes more than a few weeks. Other dimensions that could be considered are things even as simple as how the technology is perceived by its populace. A nation of haters and doomers is unlikely to advance AI or even benefit from any actual AI tools that do deliver.

## Things I am hoping to see

I would like to see an open source AI router of some sort. One that can evaluate user input and correctly assess what AI tool is required to get the best result. As many have been saying for some time, you do not need a PhD level AI to do the weekly shop or order a list of things to do. However, such tools seem to be quite rare. Even the current candidates for such tools are lacking in many respects. This is an interesting issue as here we can see that there is an alignment problem. The AI vendors want you to spend as much money as possible. The user wants to spend the least amount of money possible. How this is reconciled is an interesting problem. One that the larger players are not well motivated to resolve. There are also significant issues in how to even approach this type of issue. For instance, just how does the evaluation of user input for an AI typed query work in reality? It would require computation and as such will incur a cost. Local AI solutions can negate some of these but do not remove them altogether. There is a lot more I can say on this topic but for now I think my point is made. We need and will hopefully see such things become more common this year.

A better AI solution is required at nation state levels. It is obvious to see that relying on technologies being developed by other nation states is only going to make a nation weaker rather than stronger. Just how long will it be before everyone is calling the Gulf of Mexico the Gulf of America instead. Will the "ownership" of the Falkland Islands be reliant on what an LLM states as true? This presents a whole quagmire of potentially contentious details that would need to be worked out. It is not even unreasonable to say that some of these issues will continue to be more than a little difficult to resolve. But the point remains, these are issues that need to be addressed for each and every nation state.

AGI. There I said it. But from this point forward I think that we now have all of the tools necessary to create AGI. I would also state that there is no singular solution to the problem of intelligence. It is well accepted that there are different forms of intelligence. The way different humans solve problems is not the same every time. There is more than one solution that can and does occur. As a caveat to this I would add that although all the tools are present which are likely required to give us AGI that they may not be as yet mature enough to deliver. But it only takes a few "eureka" moments and that could easily change. This is not to say that it will happen, just that it can.

One thing I would really like to see is a set of UK AI tools. We seem to keep inventing things here but then fail to develop it. Instead it gets sold off for pennies on the dollar in many instances. Then, for some reason, there is a definite will to push AI use in virtually all areas. Yet not on UK solutions or platforms. This seems to be a track that will only end badly, for UK residents at least. Even though such a thing would be more expensive we would actually be buying something else. Security. By having our own AI infrastructure we are better protected from the influences of others. This is also advice I would give to any other nation.

Material science is going to continue to see new recipes and fabrication techniques. I am even hoping that biological production methods are improved. What would happen to Nvidia's value if we develop a technique that uses organic systems where we can just grow the computational substrate needed? I mean nature has already "solved" this problem to a degree and with the likes of CRISPR such solutions seem less fantastical. This also leads onto photonic and analogue computing. I am very keen on the idea of using the very nature of the universe as a computational device. I mean technically we do now, but just not all the possible dimensions that we can. Certain systems have significant advantages over others when applied to certain types of problems. It feels that we are being inefficient by not using all of the tools at our disposal.

More serious conversations on resource distribution. As I have said more than once over the years, we live on a water planet. Why does anyone die of thirst? AI will continue to encroach on societies as a whole. Already we have seen 2025 be a year of nothingness in some terms. For instance trying to get a job as a recent graduate in some industries is nigh on impossible. In the UK we do not even seem to have an AI curriculum in place for our education system. Instead we are still teaching our young how to live in a society that will no longer exist by the time they graduate. This to my mind is a massive political failing. Politics is a technology that is supposed to solve a set of problems. Yet it now seems to be a self serving system that is blind to the actual needs of people and instead follows what is trending on the social media platforms they consider important. This is simply not sustainable in the long term.

This truly is the time of the builder. 2026 is going to see some amazing software creations. I would even love to see a return to efficiency. Not every program should need 1GB of RAM and 30GB of diskspace to run. It saddens me greatly to see such bloat. It screams of people not knowing what they are doing. There are problems that should be solved problems in many respects — things that are best done in a particular way, for instance.

## Other Predictions

Already we have seen signs of litigation in regard to agents acting on behalf of their users and therefore skipping adverts and similar. To counter the threat of agents they are stating that they should not be used on their websites. Is this the first hate crime against automatons? Foolishness aside, this is a trend that we are going to see. Equally we will see innovative attempts to create new and more defensible revenue streams. AI is being disruptive in this sense. More so than seems to be reported on. I have written before about having AI systems that pre-process any information we consume to keep out unwanted adverts. How is that stopped? Exactly!

Just how much is the conversation you have with an LLM even worth in the form of training data? I think I could argue that they can become significant. Perhaps that will be a vocation of the future. A professional position of being able to create novel sets of long form data. And perhaps that would be the way where we could ensure that we keep thinking. I know that some people may be inclined to think less when using AI systems. But for myself I would have to state that it makes me think an awful lot more than I used to. And I can build now as never before.

With everyone now realising that just scaling LLMs is not working as hoped there will be a pivot. New methods will be applied in all manner of ways. Each one will be searching for that "perfect solution" to the problem of intelligence. And very likely there are going to be combinations that perform better than others. I have really liked the idea of the hierarchical recursive approach from the likes of Samsung and others. That functional feel to the solution just seems very natural.

## A Matter of Trust

So just who are you going to trust this year? You have the tools now to check like never before. A few clicks is often all that is needed to verify an answer. And you do not even need to rely on one LLM to assist you. Use them all if possible and practical. The see where the consensus is between multiple models. You can examine every set of Terms and Conditions that most would blindly just accept and see if there are any negative or overreaching elements that are detrimental to you. It is just so easy now and is only set to become even easier.

So 2025 gave us the tools to determine our own truth and 2026 will be our opportunity to verify that truth in order to know who to trust. This hits on two of my core principals, "Trust but Verify" and "Follow the data".
