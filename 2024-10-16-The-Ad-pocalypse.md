# The Ad-pocalypse

Adverts are a part of our life that for many of us we just deal with. Whether we like them or not it is undisputable that they work. At least enough to become as pervasive as they have. There are many aspects to advertising that are questionable when it comes to ethical considerations. After all the very nature of the profession is to steer human behaviours to a point that is advantageous to the provider. Sorry I am sure I meant to say something more like "promote an equitable exchange that is beneficial to all parties".

Being able to shift people’s opinions is a powerful thing. To convince consumption of one version of a product or service over another. Even the very act of consumption itself can be encouraged by an advertising master. As we all know, "with great power comes great responsibility". To add to that, "power corrupts, absolute power corrupts absolutely".

Looking at the history of marketing reveals considerable efforts in honing the techniques required to encourage people to act in a particular way. The ingenuity and divisiveness would put most Bond-esque super villains to shame. Much of the technology is not even revealed to the casual consumer. That nice, pleasant aroma that hits you as you enter a shop, well that is often a designed smell. It can even be added to the air ventilation system. The reason being that smells can stimulate our brains in ways that promote certain types of behaviours. This can then be coupled with other stimuli to encourage real actions in the World. That mirror in the store in a fitting room is designed very specifically to make you look "better" than in reality. By devious positioning and subtle curving, you can add a thinning effect to the reflection observed. That new outfit you got as you thought it made you look slimmer; it is not the outfit.

Technology has allowed for new ways to be created to assist the modern marketing function. But there is a possible future where the practical space for marketing to operate no longer exists.

## The "Algorithm"

Frequently this is inferred. A special piece of maths function that defines who you are. There is not just one, there are plenty. One for Google, one for Netflix, another for Amazon and a host of others. I have passed many an hour with the deliberate pollution of someone's electronic definition. Just one episode of "The Power Puff Girls" or “Baby Shark” is enough to defeat the supposed AI present in establishing recommendations. Appalling churlish behaviour that is likely unforgivable, but still funny.

Gifts have always proven to be difficult. Just one purchase and you can be suddenly exposed to a whole new world of consumption that you were unaware of. This is compounded further by loyalty cards and a stream of emails asking you to buy more things.

Of course, this is only one very narrow example of modern marketing and there are many more. There are even instances of major technology companies performing what was considered unethical human experimentation in the development of new marketing tools and technologies. Given the extremes that some are willing to go to should indicate only too well the value of the prize they are after.

## A Solution of Sorts

Ad blockers have been around as long as there have been adverts on the internet. Initially little more than a script to intercept a message using some regular expression. The advertisers then defeated early scripts by the judicious use of sneaky characters such as "space" or substituting a letter for a number. Human ingenuity never ceases to amaze me. Regular expressions were improved upon, and so it begins.

The arms race between the two has been relentless in many respects. "Bad" advertising has been used as a mechanism to defraud people a lot. So much so that certain service providers were quite happy to support and even recommend solutions that helped temper the advertising madness that can be expected on an unfiltered internet. This was not because the advert middlemen were coy in taking advertising money from business folk with less than sincere intentions. Have you ever heard of a case where an advertising distributor has handed back money spent by a scammer as they considered it immoral to keep it? The reason that advertisers were happy to support and even promote ad blocking solutions is that it reduced their liability and risk. Many court or government questions could be batted away by stating that a free solution was available for the advert problem, and that it was even offered support by the means of promotion on distribution platforms such as Google Play.

But this is only ever a temporary respite no matter what side of the marketing arms race you are on. Even legislation that is supposed to protect consumers is mired in complexity of international law and borders. The wheels of justice sometimes turn at a glacial rate.

## End Users Are Stealing!

A quandary of sorts is that I always felt as if advertising companies were not strictly honest as they could be. For myself, I tend to know what I want to purchase well in advance. In that respect I would say that I have a well-defined customer profile on many sites. So why am I at times presented with adverts that just do not seem to align to any part of my purchasing habits or interests? This is relatively anecdotal and for years I have used ad blockers. But at times I am on new systems where such things are not viable for compliance reasons, so I soon get to see how things currently are.

In little time I am presented with adverts that have no relevance to me. Some are even tailored to look like search results with the intention to get you to click on them. If this should happen, then the advert distributor will make a charge against the company who placed the advert. All parties have consented to such action of course. All are willing participants. The end user using a search engine of some kind. The advert provider providing the search service gets paid by selling advertising space to product and service providers.

Of late (June, 2024) Google has been making sounds of preventing adblockers from working on YouTube. The reason for this is that people who use adblockers are stealing from YouTube revenues. The monsters! It was this move that provoked me to consider the question of advertising a little more. Thanks Google!

## There Is No Limit

Technology has advanced to such a degree that it is now possible to see almost anything you want. Sure, there are still errors and it is still very much a work in progress. But given where we are now compared to two years ago is simply astounding. Two years from now and we will have the most amazing dynamically generated images, movies, music and more.

But what of advertising when it is from a person you care for trying to sell you a thing you know you do not want or need? As perverse as that may seem it is a possible prospect. I can have a sense of you, and then start using Open Source Intelligence techniques to find someone who I can digitally clone. Perhaps to even use personal loss and tragedy as a means of manipulation. What if a deceased loved one was contacting you from beyond the grave as you had not bought the right brand of washing powder...?

As perverse as some of this may seem it is grounded very much in history. The lengths that some people will go to in order to sell you something that can kill you seems to have no bounds. Even babies are not safe from the likes of Johnson & Johnson when it comes to talcum powder according to court filings. Microbeads of plastic in soap, what could go wrong?

## Dead Marketing

There are sites that exist that seem to defy logic or reason. One such site is LinkedIn. The usefulness is obvious to all, at least initially. But soon the system becomes "gamed". When this happens the utility of the site as a whole is reduced. Often the reduction is not immediately fatal, but it is indicative of a future trend that should be resisted. Second Life, MySpace, Twitter and others have had their time in the sun before seeing a less certain or niche future.

One of the concepts spoken of in computing circles for years has been the idea of a "Dead Internet". This is where all the content on the internet is created and consumed by bots and AI agents of various types. So just who gets paid if your AI Agent looks at an advert for you? How do you even know that it is an AI Agent? This is what LinkedIn feels like for me in many ways now. Feeling obliged to use it and in some respects, it is very useful. But also, there is an awful lot of tat that is self-serving or worse. Not wanting to pick specifically on LinkedIn here but it is a great example of how the dead internet works.

In the future, being tired of having to maintain my LinkedIn presence, I have created an AI agent that looks after it for me. The agent has a myriad of little functions that it can perform that are well defined and carry little risk. It will go off and see what skills are being sought after, do analysis of future technology requirements in all manner of disciplines. To even provide a training or material review plan for me based on what the AI Agent encounters. Little maps of how my connections are connected and much more. So why do I need to use LinkedIn, and in that I mean in the active sense and not the passive sense as offered when you have an AI Agent? The answer is that I don't. It is just a thing that happens, and I can ignore. So why advertise on LinkedIn...?

There are plenty of other sites that suffer from similar problems. Frequently they turn into echo chambers of sycophants unwilling to turn from their idols. Often destined to spiral into obscurity after a flash of action. Even now a simple "bot" can be written to echo positive supporting statements as attested to by many an Amazon review. Bots creating content for other bots to view and comment upon. Perhaps it will end in space war as with this article from 2019 by Brendan Drain. Only time will tell.

## Point Of No Return

Here is how things may work in the future. Or to put it another way, as soon as I have time to build this, I will build it, but I am very busy with building other things at this time.

My computer interface in the future will be Augmented Reality (AR). Not because I think that Reality or Virtual Reality are necessarily inferior in any way. But more of AR being that sweet spot between all of the possible realities. Part of my local compute system will consist of filters that I set for the World around me. If I have a particular aversion or attraction to certain experiences, then I can fill my day with them or remove them as I see fit. If I detest a particular thing such as fish pie then I need never encounter it again, given certain conditions.

My local compute has been configured to remove advertising data from my experiences. This even includes advertising from walking down a street. Any detected advert is simply removed or replaced. There is no discussion in this. There is no end user license agreement or any other mechanism that can be used to subvert my natural behaviours. Walking in a supermarket will become quite the bland experience. Lots of soft, clean colours with clearly labelled product information with my AI health assistant chipping in to comment on what should be bought.

What will advertisers be able to do to combat this? This type of future means that advertising as we currently know it will no longer exist. Will advertisers turn to different activities in order to promote their goods and services? I mean people could argue about such sporting events as Formula 1, but my AR glasses remove any and all references as I desire.

Some will turn to ensuring that their potential to advertise is retained in the delivery by some mechanism. There is talk of your TV watching to see when you get up to make a cup of coffee where it will then play adverts. But what about a program that stops that from happening? Please do remember that the Nvidia CEO has already declared that anyone can be a programmer in the future with just a simple prompt. This means that everyone will be a programmer and can just issue the command to build where the Nvidia AI Agents will write the code, test it and deploy it.

## Consequences of Our Actions

The result of this is that at times I could easily miss an unmissable deal. Or a once in a lifetime opportunity. The flip side to this though is that now that I am less distracted by adverts and have more liquidity, I can use my time and resources doing something else. Perhaps, as I slow down later on in life, I may not run quite as much and take a metal detector out with me instead. Perhaps I may focus on finding treasures of old.

Just how are search engines going to support themselves in the future? To deny the utility of googling is ridiculous. Admittedly it is not quite the same experience as when Google were a little more "Don't be Evil". But Google dropped that back in 2018; "Evil is back on the menu boys!". The tidal nature of give and take, altruism and narcissism and many others are plain to see over time. But just what will Google become in the future? In fact, any platform which uses marketing as a means to be profitable. What is the point of using clickbait headlines if there are only costs associated to the activity? How can you even convert these actions into actions favourable to your company?

Additional costs also should be considered. All the extra compute power required does not come for free. Even just running and maintaining systems has a cost associated with it. So, the systems being used to stop the adverts from unwanted companies has an environmental impact. Perhaps people will see this as a sign of marketing being over for green industries? As I am not sure how they can close the loop on what seems to be a conflict. In fact this has puzzled me for years; why do supermarkets spend so much on colours, materials, advertising and more to tell us how environmentally aware that they are? Surely all those packing materials and inks have an environmental impact. Is it a case of "Do as I say and not as I do"?

Irrespective of just how exactly technology progresses, and even the rules that govern our lives, there is going to be significant disruption to the marketing industry. When you look at the number of people employed in marketing roles and the effort required in that industry you should also be aware of the impacts of that whole industry disappearing. And this will not be over a period of decades. The change will come at a rate that many will find unsettling. Change is happening so fast in many industries that a single year of advancement feels like a decade or more in many fields. Advancements are only going to accelerate as we apply the new tools and skills of today.

## So It Ends

There we have it. The death of advertising as we currently know it. I have no doubt that new methods will be created to deliver "valuable product and/or service messages" to us. This will continue to be the case all the time that we have the forms of production and distribution that are based on scarcity of some form. Equally there will always be people who will accept a "free device" that is configured to play adverts from a particular provider.

Such wearable technology is still some way off as yet. Although there have been significant advances in AR technology it is still lacking in many respects. Size, performance, cost and more are issues that prevent widespread use at this time. But the advancements in AI technologies have greatly increased the attention on AR platforms. Equally AI technologies have greatly accelerated the rate of research and development in many fields. Wearable computing devices are likely to become quite sophisticated in time. Even to the point of integration into our clothing and being powered by the natural movement of our bodies in the day. Fanciful ideas for certain and yet becoming more plausible day by day. Truly amazing times.
