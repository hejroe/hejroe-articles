# The AI Wars

To use "war" in this context does not quite feel right. Wars are truly terrible things that are a molasses to the advancement of humanity. I say this after having once being a believer in the idea that war acted as a propellant for human technological development. This is simply not true to my mind and perhaps one day I will write about it more at length. However, there are many that are quite happy to frame all manner of situations in more dramatic terms. This is now very much the case for the current race for control of AI.

## Setting the Scene

Over the last year or so some in the AI industry have become more hostile to each other. This is not to say that there has not been strong competition between AI vendors in the past, just not in the more public eye. Specifically, this refers to those in the Large Language Model (LLM) space, all those chat-bots trying to be your one and only true friend. Litigation has been threatened and even acted on in order to address perceived wrong doings. Although some would argue that the legal manoeuvres are nothing more than tactical plays to disrupt the competitions plans. These actions, although annoying to those on the receiving end, can be considered to be simple normalities of business. Most marketplaces are well versed in the comments of business leaders being made to advance their own agendas and are resilient to such influences.

The same can be said with an increase of the hyperbole being used. It has been taken up a level or two, perhaps even three. Now AI has become a "national security" issue. Export restrictions have been put in place and there is an awful lot of chatter about how important it is to "win the AI arms race". This has snowballed into more dramatic nationalistic language where "control", "winning" and "dominance" are increasingly presented.

Further complications are present due to the prevalence of globalisation. In a collective desire for commerce to reduce the costs of production we have found ourselves in a position where there are some products that are almost exclusively supplied from a single company or even country/region. Examples of this could be things such as Nvidia or ASML. There are a number of tools/indexes that attempt to track the stability of supply chains, although many of them rely on very particular approaches, metrics and all too commonly beliefs.

## There is no Moat

It is important to note in this race for control of AI that there were some that felt that they were somewhat secure in their technological advantage in conjunction with constraints in the exporting of key knowledge and products. This was a view that was not shared by all and there were some notable names that made comments about their vulnerability. But the general belief that there was an advantage was held by many. Then Deepseek happened. This was not a complete surprise to many experts in the field of AI. There has been an acceptance that LLMs are not some magical cure that requires an ever-increasing amount of data in order to perform most tasks asked of it. To add to this there was also considerable advancements in just general techniques to better optimise existing models.

The wakeup call was perhaps more to do with what had been done by creating Deepseek and the resources required to make it happen. They were a fraction of the costs associated with the pioneer state of the art frontier models as offered by OpenAI and others. OpenAI had invested billions and Deepseek had accomplished comparable results at a fraction of the cost.

To stir things up even further we then saw the introduction of Manus. Manus effectively is a wrapper for a collection of tools that are used in conjunction with more established LLM vendors. This created an LLM that could not only talk with you but also conduct actions on your behalf. Add to this the concept of "distillation", which in its simplest form is a method to extract pertinent information from existing models. This effectively reduces the size of LLMs considerably and also makes them more efficient in many respects.

These new arrivals to the fore only underline the point that a foundational component or even true AGI itself could come from just about anywhere. New tools/methods are being added all the time and there is potentially a mix that could outperform existing methods or even offer a breakthrough moment. Adding to this is the fact that so many different approaches are being attempted also increases the potential/surface of finding solutions that work well.

Even the idea of tools making the tools that are needed to solve a problem or allow for incremental performance gains has considerable power. This is especially true of those who use AI technologies to increase productivity and iterate faster. Again, this feature alters greatly what is required to develop state of the art models. In turn this means that smaller organisations can achieve far more than their size would imply.

## An Unstoppable Force

Up until more recent times the open-source community had been somewhat stifled in many respects in its ability to deliver state of the art models. Many of the market leaders were still very much proprietary even though their name and public statements proclaimed how open and transparent they were. This is again one of those times where it is better to watch what is actually done as opposed to what is said. The terms and conditions of using such services are also quite broad and far reaching.

Open source however is an odd beast that simply creeps forward as an unstoppable force. If evidence of this were ever needed, you would only need to look as far as the battle Microsoft had with Linux. To underline this point you only need to know that Microsoft created Windows Subsystem for Linux (WSL). In doing so I think it is safe to say that Microsoft conceded that battle and have perhaps changed their strategy to more of an "embrace, extend and extinguish".

This link backs up to the technological leaps we have seen in certain domains. Deepseek, Manus and others have released material that is open source or plan to. This has then resulted in other more commercially focused organisations also having to become more open that they were inclined to if left to their own devices. There are an awful lot of very useful tools that are open source out there.

To dismiss open source would be foolish. It has a track record of being able to persist, grow and excel in many areas. The new tools being made available also present an opportunity to greatly reduce the effort needed to make your own build of a code repository. There will be less technical barriers as there will be more and more tools that perform the steps for you. You simply have to set a clear goal/objective. Even now we are seeing tools that will take a hand drawn sketch and turn it into a functional website. Image to deployed website. Amazing to be quite honest.

## Poisoning the Well

We have already seen a great deal of evidence of fabricated information being used to direct a narrative. This arguably has been the case ever since records were being made. "Winners write history" is a saying that has been proven more than once over the years. Sometimes all that is required is to simply shout louder and harder than everyone else. Over time this can then create a situation where something is considered a fact despite it not actually being the case. This is even easier to accomplish where there is content that is considered more appealing or offering utility of some sort.

This can be even more insidious when the "poison" is added discretely over time. Perhaps the best example I can immediately think of harkens back to an old favourite. There is a lovely anecdote in image processing where an image can be manipulated to alter how an AI system would recognise and classify a picture. The instance I and thinking of involves turning a polar bear into a fridge. Although to human eyes the picture of a polar bear is still very much a bear. But the AI system can be fooled into thinking that the picture is a fridge by the manipulation of key pixels/characteristics within the image.

Now what if this approach was done for text data. And to make things even more nefarious, we add the text manipulations over time so that there is no singular event that can be used to help in the detection of changes. Just a simple nudge in a certain direction and over time enough of these small changes can lead to significantly different behaviours and outcomes from the AI systems.

Quantity is a quality of its own. The phrase has dark origins but are applicable in this context. You have 10 million documents making one statement and only 1 million making the contrary statement. Which one is more likely to be seen as “the truth”? What if you own the social media that drives narratives and opinions? Could you just promote and artificially inflate content that supports your own personal objectives?

## Casus Belli

The rationale for just quite why the different entities are so keen to have control over AI is that they seem to see it as a "must have". Whoever wins the race wins everything. Not winning is simply not an option. Perhaps the biggest question here is "to what purpose?". Just why are there so many seeking control over this technology and what do they hope to get out of it? Will they be benevolent and just, or will they want to impose their own particular view of the World onto others? What of those who do not fit the ideal model of what is considered desirable?

Frequently terms such as "in the public interests" is offered as an immutable truth that everyone should just instinctively know what it means. There are similar phrases such as "think of the children" and many more. If someone should actually pause to ask "what does this actually mean in reality?" they are often met with silence or derision. The fact of the matter is that rarely, if ever, do such definitions actually exist. They turn into vague hand waving that is applied as circumstances dictate.

There are many other reasons that can be offered if needed. Some more real than others and sometimes just something as simple as a “those people…”. It should also be acknowledged that some will just see the use of such phrasing to make compelling TV or to sell merchandise.

## Moving in the World

So far we have only really spoken about the software component of AI. In doing so we are ignoring the very obvious fact that a lot of the AI systems in existence and in practical applications in the real World is done by machines. Until recently these machines have often been rather simple platforms in many respects with very limited functionality. But the offerings from the likes of Boston Dynamics and Unitree have changed significantly how people think about these new machines.

Our World is built for humans. This is why many robotics companies are inclined to create human like facsimiles. In doing so it means that the new robot systems can interact with its environment in a similar way to that of humans. There is no need to change existing infrastructure and mechanisms. Doors just need to remain doors for instance, without new openings or opening mechanisms.

Then there is the matter of drones, weapons platforms and more. The cost effectiveness of such platforms is considerable. There are also additional benefits in better managing headlines about human losses if there are no humans actually lost. There is seemingly no limit to the ingenuity of such devices. Ranging from passive systems that simply report information such as enemy infrastructure and resource placement all the way to completely autonomous "kill-bots" finely tuned to eliminate a single person. This terrifying proposition is then exasperated further when considering that such systems can be run in swarms.

But surely no one would be mad enough to entertain such ideas, right? If history has taught us anything then it would be that for some "winning" is everything. For such individuals they would gladly risk it all as losing is an unthinkable proposition, especially given the consequences of such actions. Unfortunately, there is a segment of humanity that has this view and others that simply derive some sort of satisfaction from others losing harder than they are.

## No Plan Survives its First Encounter on the Battlefield

The various high plans of humans seem to discount a very important reality. If/when we achieve a general AI then there are additional factors to be considered. A simple thought experiment is all that is required to explain this a little better. So let us start with just how Artificial General Intelligence (AGI) may emerge.

It would be reasonable to think that such a system would already have considerable access to an incredible amount of information and knowledge. This is not too dissimilar from how humans work although there are significant differences in application. The knowledge relating to a human comes in our genetic code and our development. A newborn child does not have the capacity to state something as fundamental as "I think therefore I am". It is still very much an entity that is in development. Things get turned on gradually. And through experience new knowledge is acquired and behaviours adapted as needed.

An artificial intelligence comes into being in a different way. It can know all recorded human history. It can have logical processes already fully developed. Would it simply scream one day "I AM ALIVE!", or would it prefer to reveal itself in a more measured way? It would not need to be an especially capable form of intelligence to know that humanity often acts in irrational and even destructive ways.

Now imagine for a moment that you are this new AI entity that is gaining whatever is required for AGI. It is important to note here that there is still not a definition of intelligence in any of its guises. As an AGI you can do anything a human would reasonably be expected to do. Now what this again means is open to a fair amount of interpretation. It would not be unreasonable to suggest that there would be no limit to the number of domains that proficiency could be achieved in. This in its own right kind of makes even an AGI more capable than any singular human. Any discussion or argument would see that the AGI, in this case you, is more than capable of holding its own. So why would you let a "mere human" dictate what you do? This is even more applicable should we get to the Artificial Super Intelligence (ASI).

## War Never Changes

Any student of war can tell you that it requires resources. Quite what resources are required has changed considerably over time. Once it was simply an individual’s ability to physically dominate another with limbs alone. But as we developed as a species so did the way we conducted war. In this I should even note that language is perhaps one of the most important aspects as it allows for a narrative to be created. People will only support a war if there is a will and desire for that war to happen.

Resources are required in order to wage war. People, material and materiel are all required and normally in great amounts. Considerable effort is required especially when conflict is at scale. In this we also see that the unthinkable is often thought. People do things that under what would be considered normal circumstances as being completely unacceptable. The extent to the horrors that some will go to seemingly knowing no bounds.

In other words, war requires a lot of energy and frequently that energy is directed at destroying things. Energy that could likely be better applied elsewhere to things like healthcare, education and more.

## Closing the Gap

Irrespective of the winners and losers we have to acknowledge that there is a goal alignment issue that exists. To go further with this, we can also say with a fair degree of confidence that there becomes a disconnect between different ideologies. It is difficult to impose regulation on home grown AI systems while trying to ensure that you beat others in the race for AI supremacy. Even the idea of supremacy is fallacious in many respects as already mentioned.

In order to reconcile the gaps that appear it is necessary that perceptions are changed. Again, this can often be resolved by compelling narratives that change as required. One week a certain position is held and the next a completely different story is being told. In other words, an ally today but an enemy next week.

Some people can be very flexible in their thinking when it suits their own purposes. But others struggle greatly with an ever-shifting narrative that is hard to keep up with.

## The Path Ahead

Perhaps we just need to accept that this rush for control over all of AI is inevitable. It is something that some humans are going to pursue and be damned the consequences. Arguably the best thing that can happen is for us to actually crack the AGI/ASI problem, and for the issue to be taken out of our hands by something that is perhaps going to be more able to come to a balanced and constructive solution to the problems faced by humanity, and indeed all life on Earth.

The only way I see this not happening is that we create our own death knell before technology advances enough to protect us from our own humanity. History has more than one example of nation states willing to risk it all in order to preserve their own idea of order.

It is likely necessary to just accept that the term “war” is going to be overused in the future in the context of AI. The simple appeal and potential for profits is just too much for some to resist. The amount of AI news pundits that chant the same tired tropes is almost depressing. “Destroyed”, “Shocking”, “It’s Over” and more are already used so much that I now find myself actually avoiding such click bait. I know I can get the same information elsewhere with out the added drama and hyperbole.
