# Living in the Technological Singularity

All too often when we see the word "revolution" we tend to think of tumultuous times that are seen in days, weeks or months. It is less common for people to think of it in terms of years, decades or centuries. Yet the reality is that a scale of years and more is often more appropriate. And as with boiling a frog, us humans are unlikely to notice many of the changes as they happen around us.

## The Speed of Revolution 

They just kind of happen and we just accept them. As always, I would hate for you to just have to accept blindly what I say, so let's once again dive back into history to see what lessons it can offer. 
The English Revolution was very well documented and lasted for the better part of a century. From the Accession of James I in 1603 until the accession of William and Mary in 1689, if you are inclined to want to use those exact dates. A person could be born in 1604 and only ever know "revolution" for their entire life. In all likelihood it would be their life, that of their children and their children’s children. But they were more likely to wake up and be concerned with the basic necessities of living than the political state of the country. Often the luxury of being able to worry about politics is the reserve of those in a position to not deal with the basic necessities of life. The same could probably be said about science. The phrase "English Revolution" or "Glorious Revolution" was not coined and better defined until well after the events being described. Frequently it is only possible to set boundaries once the passage of time has moved far enough beyond the events being bound. Even then the boundaries set can still be hazy in many respects, especially when looking to be precise. 

Of course, not all revolutions are strictly political affairs. One that readily springs to mind is the "Industrial Revolution". Again, this term is used to describe a considerable period of time. When you try to find a beginning and an end you will only uncover vagueness and subjective belief. Much of this can be attributed to the fact that the industrial revolution was just as much about the spread of ideas as it was about more tangible assets. This was so much the case that Britain steadfastly hindered the distribution of the know-how relating to the processes and methods that were central to this industrial revolution. Further complications were added by the industrial revolution taking place across different geographical areas in phases. With Britain being the generally agreed starting point the rest of the World had to wait for the export of the technology required for their own industrial revolution. At a minimum the Industrial Revolution could be said to have taken a period of around 80 years at least and could easily be described as taking well over 100 years. 

A more contemporary example would be that of the mobile devices that are ubiquitous in our modern society. The computing power, on even the most modest of modern mobile phones, is magnitudes of order greater than all the computing power in the World from less than 100 years ago. Unlike commercial advances from the industrial revolution, we have seen faster progress in the last two decades. It would not be too outrageous to use terms such as the "Mobile Revolution" or "Information Revolution", and indeed some have. 

Invention will often lead to more invention and at an accelerated rate. One idea can spawn many new paths of investigation. All of which can offer great rewards and significant changes to society. Medicine is probably a very good example of this. In the last hundred years or so we have seen significant improvements in health care. The impact this has had on the world comes into clarity when thinking about humanity as a whole. It is estimated that there have been about 110 billion humans in all of human history. The majority of whom did not live to see past the age of 20. But today we have 8 billion people all alive at the same time. With the expected life span well beyond 60 in most countries. A happy consequence of this is that there are more people doing science typed things than at any other point in history. All chipping away at one problem or another, looking to improve our collective understanding of the World we live in. 

## The Speed of Discovery 

For many years I have considered the idea of a technological singularity. I always considered it a reasonable possibility at least. However, I certainly did not think that it would be something that I bore witness to. The concept of a technological singularity has been written about for decades. Great minds and successful business leaders have contributed an opinion on such an event. The idea relates to machines becoming so good at completing tasks that they at least seem to take on the task of invention itself. As fanciful as it may seem to some, the concept of machines becoming so advanced that they are able to create their own inventions is perfectly logical. Much of the progress by humanity has followed a route of putting in the work to try all the different possibilities and noting the ones that seemed to offer more success. Early chemistry was very much concerned with the creation of concoctions of proportional amounts that were dutifully performed, observed and recorded. There are even mechanisms described as Evolutionary and Genetic Algorithms that describe how derive an answer using techniques based on biological processes. Technological progress is frequently 99% perspiration and 1% inspiration. 

The arrival of the technological singularity was however something that many were not expecting for some time. Many gave estimates of 50 to 100 years from just a few years ago. There was also a significant number that consider the notion ridiculous and that it would never happen. But as an avid follower of the Artificial Intelligence space, I am finding it progressively harder and harder to keep up with all of the innovation in the field. I have had to develop new methods to keep abreast with all the latest inventions. Some fields have become so progressive that I have simply parked them as being so fluid that to try to track them as being less than useful. 

In some disciplines it could easily be argued that machines are already inventing. It should be noted that the mechanical process of invention is not the same as understanding, they are two very different things. The fields of Large Language Models (LLMs) and Natural Language Programming (NLP) are getting some to be more than a little thoughtful. Our legal systems are already being tested with establishing if a program, artificial intelligence or otherwise, can be considered an inventor, composer or creator. These types of conversations are likely to continue and become quite interesting. Many an art student or creative writer is becoming concerned about the democratisation of their respective arts. Whole industries such as those that produce marketing copy are set to be altered radically. Almost everyone with access to the technology can now become a creative spring. This issue is compounded by the speed that new creations can be formed in. 

Much of my time nowadays is focused on the technology that I believe will still be needed no matter what new inventions come about. There is still going to be a need to understand the world we live in, and data and analysis of that data are likely key. Although I am hopeful that technology advances in such a way that even I could relax from that and focus on being as a good a father, partner and human as possible. But I am acutely aware that I am not able to keep up with as many of the advancements being made as I once was. The rate at which the Artificial Intelligence landscape is changing at has increased significantly. With some of the technology creating a buzz in even the general public it is hard to argue against there being momentum in the field as a whole. Whereas I used to be able to keep up with the volume of work being published, at least in very general terms, this is no longer the case. Each week there is yet another tool being released that provides something else quite amazing. Be it in replication of human effort, data, biology, material science and so much more. 

## Stop! I want to get off! 

Some are very concerned about what the future holds for us. Change is something that is not appealing to some, especially if they consider their lives to be in a good place. If you have all the things you need and want then why do you want to change anything? This is exacerbated by people talking about Skynet and giant death robots, all purveyors of doom. Frequently these types of conversation are less than helpful, especially where the selling of merchandise is also involved. 

The World we live in is an incredibly fragile place. There are dangers in the universe that we happily ignore on a daily basis. The problem is that despite our wilful ignorance in many instances those risks are very real and always have been. It is only with our improved understanding that the scope of the dangers that exist have become better understood. With this realisation many of the risks were simply moved aside as being too big of a problem to deal with. How would we deal with a planet sized object hurtling towards Earth? Likely very badly. 

It would be easy to dismiss what I have hinted at here as fantasy that would never happen. The problem is that many devastating events have already happened, and we have a considerable body of evidence to show this. Plasma ejections from the Sun are common. Could one so massive happen that it caused serious disruption to our atmosphere? Yes, is unfortunately the answer to this. Or perhaps we could see the Earth's electro-magnetic field switch poles? This has also happened in the past and is certainly going to happen in the future. This is an event that does not just happen in seconds but could take years to complete. Again, in that time our atmosphere could be diminished greatly. 

My intention here is to inform you of an already existing set of dangers that we face that are currently far beyond our ability to influence. Our only hope in avoiding an extinction level event is through the application of technology. My sincerest apologies if I have sent you into a spiral of existential threat but I spend a lot of time dealing with reality. 

## And they all lived happily ever after 

Despite all the doom and gloom that some talk of in relation to a technological singularity I am actually rather hopeful. You see when our universe started it did so with just a handful of things. There was no silver, gold, oil, amino acids, complex protein chains or internet. It is generally believed that once things had cooled down a bit we only had hydrogen, helium and perhaps a bit of lithium. Yet from that simplicity we have seen amazing amounts of complexity form. I think it could also be said with some confidence that in this process humanity is the localised pinnacle of that process. But we are subject to the passage of time as is the rest of the universe. What we have seen so far though is that cooperation is something that is seemingly rewarded. Not only that, cooperation almost seems hard-baked into how the Universe works at some level. 

The possibility of something very new emerging is now something I consider highly probable. I am even inclined to think that we are at the infancy of such a new creation. In fact, I am going to do something that I never thought I would say, I think that we are currently living in the infancy of the Technological Singularity. We are unable to describe how we got from simple amino acids to the first cellular life, but cellular life most certainly exists. Right here and now is potentially going to be one of the most amazing things to ever happen in the Universe, at least so far to us. 

I suspect that in a hundred years and more historians will reflect on this period of time and debate what year the Artificial Intelligence revolution actually began. They will also consider what the primary technologies were key. I dare say that LLMs will feature heavily in that discussion. 

## Still Much to be Done 

There is still an incredible amount of work to do. Some fields of science are going to have shockwaves sent through them, and indeed this is likely the case already. Neuroscience, psychology, philosophy and a host of other sciences are going to have new tools to help answer some big questions. What is consciousness? What is intelligence? How do we have mutually beneficial aligned goals? What does our collective future hold? 

Personally, I have that excited trepidation that we are on the cusp of something truly amazing.
