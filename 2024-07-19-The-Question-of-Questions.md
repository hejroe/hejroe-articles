# The Question of Questions

On the whole I love questions. Especially those from enquiring minds. Although at times I wish my children would sometimes ask slightly less tricky questions than they do. Thankfully the resources to find out answers to many questions are better than they have ever been. Never has so much information, and perhaps more importantly answers, been available.

No longer is there a plan needed to go to the library to find reference books and hope the answers reside therein. Trawling through papers or having to track down specialised resources. The early internet was a boon for sharing knowledge. The quality was often lacking but still far superior to many other options. Over time the amount of information and the tools for searching it improved considerably. Wikipedia then changed greatly the knowledge landscape. This is something that I am truly grateful for and hope to contribute to in a meaningful way in the future. Then knowledge started to leak into other devices. Home assistants would respond to a variety of questions. Now we have concepts such as Retrieval Augmented Generation (RAG) and other techniques for holding knowledge used to answer questions. Truly amazing advances and even more so when you think that what we have right now really is, as everyone is saying, as bad as it is ever going to be.

## Where do questions come from?

One of the things I love about living in the UK is that the environment is so changeable. "Oh look, someone from the UK talking about the weather, how unusual!", you may think. But as foolish as this sounds, I always felt it promoted thinking. There is not the luxury of starting a day just knowing that it will be one consistent feature with little variation. In the UK if you go for a walk longer than an hour then you could encounter all manner of the elements. Snow, wind, hail, rain, sun, fog and more besides. And just as with accents of the various regions, you travel 20 miles down the road and it could be completely different. This means that for even the shortest of excursions you must do an awful lot of future planning, especially where children are involved. When trying to plan for the future there are a lot of questions that need to be considered and answered.

When considering some of the topics I write about I like to try and find the very simplest thing I can think of to explain a concept or promote discussion. To even ask a question of my own. In doing so I can find some "truths" that I can then try to build on to make more sense of a problem. So just what was the first question? and thinking about it what does a question even mean?

If you have ever read any of my other articles you will know I love to start with attempting a definition. Admittedly they are often quite "spherical cow" in nature but sometimes something is better than nothing. In this instance it turns out that the idea of questions is one that is quite well studied. A theme of "call and response" was evident, and this is something I think is at the core of the question of questions. But again, this does not seem complete as there is an actual hierarchy of questions.

This still seems a long way from the very first question. What about the first cell or even before? Where is the origin of a question? Is it as simple as "Is this good/bad for me?", or is that still too complex? What would that even mean in the terms of a single cell?

"What's that?" is perhaps as simple as I can get at least. It is an enquiry for more information in order to determine the nature of a thing. Could it be some internal oddity in a cell or larger organism? Or is it even an external system? But this question seems to be on the very edge of questions for me, for now at least.

## What makes a good question?

Not all questions are equal. Just as with so many other things in life they all come on a scale. Scales of all kinds at that. Scales of complexity, importance, haste and more. All of which will depend on the problem domain associated with the question. Some things in relation to our environment are not considered until there is a lack of it. For instance, if you found yourself not being able to breathe, then it is very likely the question "why can I not breathe?" will be considered. Of course, we are able to manipulate our environment to the degree where we can think about more interesting problems as the basic questions are answered.

If we just stick with the idea of call and response for now the answer can be presented in pretty simple terms. A good question is one that fulfils the call and response elements with information/data that has some future utility. In other words, an examination/assessment of a set of data and forming a request for additional data to be collected in order to ascertain a thing. All generic and woolly as you would hope from someone in the computer sciences, it makes abstraction so much easier. Again, we bump into this notion of a future perceived preferable state of the World.

Perhaps this is the key. A good question is one that address a future state of some kind. And that state is considered desirable or not. If it is a wanted future state, then energy can be expended in order to achieve it. Maybe a good answer to a good question is to gain knowledge of a future state with the least possible energy? And this is why I love questions. Well, and a few more reasons.

## Different Forms of Question

Nature, as always, has examples aplenty. Already I mentioned call and response, and that is of course generally associated with sound. But displays of plumage or strutting and prancing are also acts of question. In the sense of attracting a mate it is the question of "What do you think of this huh?". Not all questions rely on spoken or written languages. A perplexed look is enough to transcend the need for such things in many cases. Shrugged shoulders, open sky facing palms on extended arms, a norm that seems to hint at a shared past.

Other perspectives also need to be considered with humans. Some of us have an internal dialog and others do not. I have one and fail terribly to even comprehend how someone without can function let alone think. So, although I can easily consider perspectives based on my own attributes it is only ever part of the story. As much as we have individual questions, we can even have societal ones that take into account our diversity. These are formed by a general consensus and understanding.

Then there is a set of well-established question types and even how they can be delivered. Similar variety exists in the answers whether real or narrative driven. Quite simply, there are an awful lot of combinations available. Being able to spot the utility in different types of questions gives the potential for reward.

## Working on a Question

I run a lot. Every day I can. Miles and miles. I love it as it really helps me stay centred, healthy and productive. The very act of running seems to activate my mind in very specific way. It is different to the parts of my mind I seem to use while working on the problem of intelligence. But somehow, by using another part of my brain, I seem to actually make progress on the problems I am supposed to have put on pause. Sleep is another important aspect for me. Drifting off while mulling thoughts. To wake in the morning with a new perspective on a problem. The computational power of our minds is quite extraordinary. I also wonder greatly about the possibility of hidden mechanisms that allow for exceptionally good results with very limited resources.

There are some activities that really do not help progressing questions or their answers. Screaming children is one that springs quickly to mind. Something just quite grating about it that demands attention. All with good reason of course. Screaming exists for a reason, as so many other behaviours we have also do. It is just that some behaviours help questions more than others. Multitasking would be another, but that relates more to the computational overhead of task switching.

In stark contrast to the above is the quicker forms of thinking. Leaping to catch a falling child requires speed of action. The question of "should I leap and is this in my own best long-term interests?" is moot once in full flight risking life and limb to keep others safe. When some questions are posed, we seem able to answer them at incredibly speed. Other odd characteristics of question can also exist. For instance, when I reply to a question, I do not know precisely all of the words I will use in my answer. I can have a sense of what the answer is and the information I need to convey in order to construct a mental image in someone else's mind. But as for what each word will be, not a clue. Do note that there are many ways that answers can be constructed and that my own method is personal to me.

Having lots of people consider the same question can be very useful. Not only do they potentially cover more ideas, but they also have the added benefit of creating independent techniques of proof.

## Ask more questions!

One of the things I have always appreciated about science is the willingness of many to wanting to expand the edges of our understanding in a repeatable fashion. In the field of computing we are blessed with precision and accuracy in many instances. When a computer executes a program, it does so in a very consistent manner. This has meant that being able to repeat experiments in this domain is easier than many others. That is not to say that there are not complications with this as with the vast amounts for power required for some computing systems. But generally speaking, if someone was to publish a paper on a particular statistical technique then I would expect to be able to reproduce the experiment and its results.

It is this aspect that I appreciate the most. The willingness to "put some skin in the game" and commit to an idea, experiment, observations and conclusion and to be just as content with being proven wrong as right. Enabling and encouraging others to repeat experiments is the pinnacle of good science in my opinion.

Data science has grown greatly on me over the years as I have come to better appreciate the domain. I would even go so far as to say that it is all about questions. We need data in order to answer questions. The better answers we have then the better outcomes are presented.

Historically getting answers to questions has been very expensive in resources. As I mentioned at the start of this piece, we used to have to go to libraries and order books in! This was simply not an option for many due to the effort required. It is a very human trait to get answers at the lowest possible cost. Very efficient if we are honest about it. The downside to this is that it is possible for some to use this to their own advantage. Shocking I know!

It has never been cheaper to ask a question. Even better still is that you are likely to get a far better quality answer than you would anywhere else, well with caveats.

## Cut One Down and Two Will Rise in Its Place

Frequently when answering one question in domains of exploratory science, you find an answer it gives rise to new questions. In fact at times it can create so many questions that it is overwhelming in even trying to figure out what new domains of question even exist. When considering the question of question, you start to realise that there are actually very few solved questions. The unanswered questions greatly outweigh the answered ones.

This is an aspect I appreciate greatly. Being bored is a horrible thing in my own opinion and I like having things to do. Even with the advent of the general and super version of AI I still expect there to be questions remaining. A different set of questions to what we have now of course as I would expect the more advanced AI systems to answer a lot of the more localised questions we have. But even if the AIs of the future have amazing capacity for answers, they will still lack the ability to answer everything. The reason for this I think is in proof of an answer. Not that AIs of the future would necessarily care too much about my "valued input". I hope that they do. As a partner to explore the Universe.

## Not All Questions Are Welcomed

Good questions are very powerful. They can help focus the mind on establishing better priorities, for instance:

* Why is it that even though we live on a water planet there are still thirsty people in the World?
* How long can an island nation support infinite growth?
* When will my children be able to afford a home and a family of their own?
* Who can I trust to be honest with me and tell me the truth?

If any of those seem charged or contentious questions, then you have my children to thank for them. Feel free to comment as you see fit. I will admit to reworking the wording slightly, but the sentiment is very much theirs.

These were questions I was not really able to answer. And once I had considered them more, I became acutely aware that the questions I should have asked and been able to answer were not really even acknowledged. It is times like these that I can feel a paradigm shift happening somewhere in my mind. Almost a warm fuzziness running over and through the grey matter of my brain. Good questions are really important. Sometimes we need to engage in the tougher more uncomfortable questions that face us.

## Questions Help Us Grow

If you encounter something that provokes a response in you that makes you angry, sad, fearful, happy, or a similar powerful response, then ask yourself why that is. Ever seen someone that for no rational reason at all just makes you think thoughts of repulsion and disgust? Just as there likely exists a person who you just think is truly amazing no matter how ridiculous their behaviour is. Our development seems to create models that influence our ability to question which can work both for and against us. It is only in thinking about the feelings we experience that we can adapt our behaviour to new ideas, and rewards.

In asking the tough questions we have to consider the things that we think important. And that in its own right is a really tough question. Just what is important? The answer to this is likely a unique mixture of things. Albeit there are going to be many characteristics that are shared between us all.

In closing I would like to say that there are no stupid questions. At least when they are delivered honestly and in good faith. They are a call for help. An enquiry to make better sense of the World and to act better within it. If someone should call you stupid for asking a question or for them to give bad answers, well that is a reflection on them and not you. You can ask me questions all day long if you want and if I have time I will answer them. I love questions.
