# The AI Wake Up Call

I seem to be on somewhat of a roll when talking about more contentious issues of late. This has never been my intention. I have only ever followed the data and listened very carefully to what is being reported. But if you have read any of my other recent pieces you will know only too well that the world we live in is changing at an unprecedented rate when it comes to the sciences. With the recent excitement about Large Language Models (LLMs) such as Chat-GPT, Bard, Bing and many others, there has been a flurry of thought leaders coming to the fore making brash statements about what should be done about AI, and frequently who is responsible.

As ever there is little agreement, and that is a perfectly normal and natural state of affairs. Much of the talk has been about how IT as a whole must take action to resolve this problem. This is more than a little interesting to me as it seems that the press at least ignore the fact that IT is not the only scientific discipline. 

Our understanding of the universe is a collective understanding. As much as some may be attracted to the idea that everything can be categorised and placed into nice tidy containers with no overlaps, the universe most definitely disagrees with this. Categorisation is a very useful tool that us humans have employed to make sense of the world, and more importantly perhaps to bend it to our will. What categorisation even is can be somewhat hard to decipher. For instance, if you were to put a collection of things in front of different people they are likely to sort them in different ways. Some may sort by colour or size and others may sort by number of legs. How categories are created seems to have no strict rule in place. There are invariably options as to how one can sort things and frequently the choice as to how this is done is subjective. 

This is the same for many other words that we use for the myriad of "obvious" tools we have. Our individual understanding seems rock solid when applying our definitions to our everyday lives. But once we are confronted with the task of actually defining in the most precise terms what something is, things soon become a bit trickier. Frequently this can lead to a spiral of circular questions asking the true nature of the thing we are looking to describe. 

For instance, there are some very large questions that seem to be continually ignored by some. Although that is probably worded a bit harsher than I intend. One that has taken up vast amounts of my own time is that of "what is intelligence?". I have looked at various definitions from dictionaries, read scientific papers from the fields of neurology, psychology, behavioural biology and more. All the time looking for something to which I can say "intelligence is.....". To this day I have not found such a text I can whole heartedly put any firm credence into. There are some lovely and elegant ideas that seem very nice when used in a narrow sense. There are also some quite unsavoury definitions used as an instrument to push certain agendas. But the overarching concept of intelligence still proves elusive. 

There is not even agreement on where intelligence is, or how we can detect it, or what unit intelligence can be measured in. Words such a consciousness or thought process are bandied about, but again each with their own issues that fail in any concrete definition. Is a bee intelligent if it can communicate with other bees about the location of resources? What about an octopus or primate? Is intelligence only present when tools are applied to bend the world to an intended shape? 

## A New Contender 

LLMs have only added to this confusion in many respects. It is hard to look at the results of LLMs and not think that the output is intelligent in some sense. In fact the Turing test only requires that the participants believe that the output generated by a computer is human. I can say with absolute certainty that many would happily believe that the output is intelligent. I myself am inclined to think that what we are seeing is some fundamental clue as to what intelligence really is. It is not truly intelligent in a way that we can think of as being alive, but there is most definitely something going on that deserves more study. 

For some this is an age of excitement. I include myself in this number. You can wake up every day and find some new work that is being pursued that is positively mind blowing. Some new method of creation, or myriad of new questions to be asked. I used to be envious of those who lived about 150 years ago. They could wake up and perform experiments and ask questions at a rate that had never been seen before. New elements and methods as to how the world worked seemed to cascade from one discovery to the next. Many of the experiments were low in cost and reasonable in effort. You could step into your garden and conduct experiments about fauna and flora as you saw fit. Much of the time you just needed to keep good records of observations. But now I find myself alive at a point in time where it is even more the case. 

## New Thinking Now 

Unfortunately, there are many that utterly fail to recognise what is happening in the AI space and rather than think in new ways they cling desperately to the old ways. They use words like stealing, morality and public interests as if they actually know what they are talking about. Frequently this is not the case. I can publish a work today and allow for it to be within the public domain. As a content creator that is my right. There is no copyright process I have to go through, I can just publish it and allow for it to be copied, used or modified. Nothing was stolen from me and I gave it freely for the interests of humanity as a whole. When I do this sort of thing it is with the intention of helping others succeed as that also serves my own interests. 

A huge part of the technology available today has been gifted by the altruistic. Those who saw the need of humanity being greater than their own personal enrichment. They published works and gifted ideas as they felt necessary. In fact, I would say it is not unreasonable to say that much of the computing we use today is within the public domain and has been for a number of years despite the efforts of corporations and governments to keep it closed. 

With the false claims from such people you have to wonder why they hold such a position. To be so wilfully ignorant or deceptive and think that they can do so without it being observed. The critical element that they fail to understand is that the collective knowledge of the world is a very complex thing that is not restricted to a particular genre, style or aesthetic. More importantly, the World is becoming better at remembering things that have been done in the past. The normal tricks of the scoundrel are becoming less effective when there is video evidence of them performing the errant behaviour. 

Thankfully their efforts are going to be futile. They may be able to sell the extra book or two, perhaps even some t-shirts but the death knell for their fanciful hyperbole will soon come to an end. They may be able to slow ever so slightly the progress that AI and its associated fields will make, but they cannot hope to halt it. 

The main reason for this is obviously apparent. No one in either the public or private sector can resist the benefits offered by the "cost savings" that AI will provide. That call to your local tax office or to the bank will invariably be handed over to AI agents. To not do so would be fiscally irresponsible to tax payers, customers and shareholders. 

I would hope that there are far more people that appreciate the potential this technological singularity has in store for us all. There is a new set of tools in which to help resolve our collective problems and to gain further insight into the world we all live in. The time of lazy and comfortable thinking in regurgitating the works of others is at an end. LLMs can do that with ease and are far more capable than their human counterparts. 

Are there many who will need to rethink how they provide for the basics of existence? Absolutely yes! This is especially true in certain fields such as "content creation" in all its guises. AI is already encroaching on many art forms and in many cases it will perform better than people. Translation is another area that will be transformed. So will customer service roles or marketing functions. Does this mean the end of humanity? Not even close. The biggest issue is that it means we have to rethink what it is to be human. We will have a chance to be elevated even further with the application of technology. Should our education system be all about being able to recite information and facts? Or should it become something where we are taught how to ask better questions? 

AI development is not going to be stopped. Perhaps the biggest danger is for one company, institution or government to hold the reigns of control for AI. The chances of that happening now though I think are incredibly slim. Too much innovation has already happened. Perhaps the best way to deploy AI is for us to individually create our own personal AI. Our own AI assistant in determining the truth using sources we can prove by simple experiments. We can each become an expert in our own lives. Connect and share as we see fit. Create and move in the world in a way that has not been possible before. 

Meaningful action is required now to address how we plan for the future. This means that people need to consider the unthinkable and to come up with plans as to how best manage our future. But AI is not going anywhere. The advantages it offers are simply too great a temptation to be ignored. This is with good reason; AI can help us all tremendously and I personally cannot wait for this to become more the case. Especially as a parent who wants to see my children have the opportunity to grow and become all that they want to be.
