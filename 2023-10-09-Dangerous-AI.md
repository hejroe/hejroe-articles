# Dangerous AI

There has already been a fair bit of discussion about the dangers of AI and I have no doubt that there will be much more to come. Keeping a reasonable sense of perspective in these discussions is going to be needed, although at times more than uncomfortable. But we need to have them none the less, to not would be a failing of our own intelligence.

Covering off the most obvious, and yet most overlooked, we are on a ball of rock hurtling around a ball of fire in space with any number of events that can remove the planet from under our feet. That is the stark reality of the situation that we currently face. Now for the bad news. The ball of fire will expand to the point where all life on Earth will be wiped out. The good news is that we have well over a billion years to work out what to do about that. But the other bad news is that there is an awful lot of other events that could hinder the efforts of life on Earth in the meantime. 

This very simple reality will not be enough to alleviate the concerns of many. There will be cries of “but AI is a real danger right now”. Clever arguments about paperclip building self-aware cellular programmable organisms or similar are presented. This is absolutely the right thing to do. But it does need to be taken into context and not just generalised away into fear, uncertainty, and doubt. 

Firstly we have to look at the definitions already offered to explain different levels/facets of intelligence. It seems that the accepted nomenclature is for “weak”, “general” and “super” when categorising intelligence. This in of itself is interesting given the fragile understanding we have of intelligence in its own right. 

“Weak AI” refers to the narrow application of intelligence. All this really means is, do one job and do it well. This is where the amazing performances from the likes of IBM’s Watson or Google’s Go-Zero projects are explained away. The counter being “they can only do tasks within a very narrow field”. 

The benchmark set for human intelligence is known as “General AI”. This is the type of intelligence that can be generalised to many varied tasks. There are features that are considered to be essential in their presence such as one or zero-try learning. This is where you get it “right” with no or very few examples, such as with identifying a face. At this time this is the very best form of intelligence we have access to. 

“Super AI” is a realm where human typed intelligence is dwarfed. This is likely the bit that can scare people the most. 

When we talk of the dangers of AI it is normally with a view that the AI will want to replace us. How this conclusion is formed has yet to be made clear to me. Normally it only goes so far as “it is what we would do”, which seems a bit of a narrow view. But for now let us keep in mind this worst-case scenario. 

So, are explosives safer now than they were in the past? In this mental leap that no one was expecting, even me, there is a point to be made. In the history of making things go bang it used to be a lot more dangerous. Nowadays modern explosives can be hammered, pounded, dropped, and moulded in complete safety. When detonated it can be done with complete control as with the demolition industries. 

The same can be said for many other aspects of human development. Is medicine safer now than it was in the past? Food production? Transport? 

The real risk for me with AI systems is already with us. It is the primitive weak AI systems that can be the most harmful. They can make or break entire governments, start wars and cause suffering on a scale never seen before. The risk made even greater by the virtue that we are woefully unprepared as a species for the rapid development that we are seeing. Many of us lack the complete set of tools to determine a truth that allows for action in our own long-term interests. Perhaps this is the real problem to be considered. Technology is advancing at such a rate that we need to adjust fundamentals such as education in order to account for it. 

Humans are very good at taking in a lot of information and acting upon it quickly. We even have special mechanisms that facilitate such things as with anger or fright. The trade off for this ability to act quickly in the world is that anger and fright tend to reduce higher brain functions rather than promote them. The same can be said for many other organisms. Speed offers rewards as well as limitations. 

The time to be warning of AI was back in the days where it was being dismissed as a thing that would never happen. “Computers will never be as good at chess as a human is” was a discussion ending argument for many years. There were others that followed based in other domains, but in essence the same core argument. 

Perhaps the best path forward is to try and progress AI to the “super” level as quickly as possible. As much as this may seem like deferring or ignoring the problem it may provide the least damaging results. There is no certainty that the result will be beneficial to all concerned, but there is considerable evidence to suggest that humans working with AI frequently yield amazing results. Far better than the results of one or the other.
